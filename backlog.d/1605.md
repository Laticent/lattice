# quadrant: one crowded slide sets the label size for every slide — and a name that doesn't fit is silently deleted

## Summary

Make the quadrant's item-label size a per-slide decision instead of one global constant, and make silently dropping a name impossible.

## Swimlane

`engineering/decisions/2026-08-10-quadrant-unit-aspect.md` — the #1544 record. §3 holds two designs that were built, rendered and rejected; §4 records the type raise that shipped and why it stopped where it did. This card is the half that decision left open.

---

## In plain terms

**What the slide is.** A quadrant is a scatter plot — dots on a 2×2 grid, each dot with its name printed beside it: "Scoring model v2", "Weekly signal brief".

**What's wrong.** Those names print at about **half the size of the page number** in the corner. From the back of a room you can see the dots but you can't read which is which — and knowing which dot is which is the entire point of the chart.

**Why nobody just made them bigger.** Bigger text needs more room beside the dot. When there isn't room, `placeLabels` (`lib/components/chart/_chart-family/svg-label.js:522`) gives up and **draws nothing** — `{ svg: '', hidden: true }`. No warning, no error, no visible overlap. The name simply isn't there.

So the size was set to the largest value where the *most crowded slide in the repo* still keeps all its names. That slide is the gallery's "Stress test — fourteen initiatives … the ceiling". When #1544 raised the size ~10%, that slide lost `Per-decision profiles` — 14 labels in, 13 out — and it was committed into a gallery PDF before the adversarial trio caught it.

**The part that's unfair.** That size is **one number in the code** (`FS.dotLabel`, `quadrant.transform.js:135`), shared by every quadrant on every slide. A slide with 4 dots and a half-empty plot prints tiny names because a different slide, in a different file, has 14.

---

## The two things to solve

1. **A chart must never silently drop a name.** "I couldn't fit this" must not mean "I'll pretend it doesn't exist." This is the real defect — a chart quietly misrepresenting the author's data, invisibly to every gate in the repo.
2. **The label size must be chosen per slide, not once for everybody.** A 4-dot slide prints big names. A 14-dot slide does something else — a numbered key down the side, or a split across two slides.

Fix those two and the original legibility complaint resolves itself: names get large on the slides with room, and the crowded ones stay honest instead of lying.

**One slide out of 27 sets the size for all 27.** Twenty-six could run at 11 or higher; most at 15–20 — 60 to 110% above what they ship at.

Against the `--fs-meta` bar (the house's smallest text tier — eyebrows, pagination — which the label currently sits at 59% of), counting slides that would clear it **if sizing were per-slide**:

| | slides at the bar |
|---|---|
| today (one global 9.5) | **0 of 26** |
| per-slide sizing, today's box | **12 of 26** |
| per-slide sizing **+ #1598's box reclaim** | **22 of 26** |

So the order of value is **per-slide sizing ≫ #1598 ≫ a key rail for the last four.** #1598 is a multiplier (it lowers the bar from 16.1 units to 13.5), not the lever.

### The four slides that sizing alone can't fix

Three cap at 11 and one at 9.5: `Crowded item names are placed apart…` (only **4** items), `Where the 18 logged decisions landed` (6 items, appears twice), and the 14-item stress slide. **Item count is not the whole story** — label *length* and dot proximity bind too, so a `capacity` cap alone won't catch these three. They are the set that needs a fallback.

## Already tried — do not retry

`2026-08-10-quadrant-unit-aspect.md` §3 rendered and rejected both:

- **(a) Widen the plot** 420×348 → 840×348. Each quadrant cell becomes ~3:1 and reads as a stretched chart, not a quadrant; costs 4:3 decks 23% of painted area and roughly halves portrait.
- **(b) Square plot + an always-on key rail.** It *worked* on legibility — key labels at **~21.8px**, above the bar, and it fixed the 4:3 case free. Rejected for the reader: every point becomes a numbered dot and reading the chart becomes a lookup.

**What was never tried is making (b) conditional.** The measurement above is the argument for it: 22 of 26 slides keep the direct name beside the dot, and only the 4 that genuinely can't pay the lookup. §3(b) was assessed as an always-on trade because no per-slide data existed at the time. `buildSvgLegend` already exists (`_chart-family/svg-legend.js`) and map, radar and quadrant's own `cohort` variant compose it.

One caution the decision doc makes explicitly: `cohort` is **not** precedent for a lookup — it names clusters at their centroids *inside* the plot and its rail carries counts, not identities. A conditional key rail would be the library's first lookup, so the fallback's threshold matters.

## Settled geometry — don't re-derive it

- **The chart is already at 100% of the height it is given** (landscape and portrait; square is width-bound). There is no fill defect — `preserveAspectRatio="meet"` is doing its job and the SVG's border box measures its container's content box exactly. Growing the plot requires growing the **box**.
- **Label placement cannot see the box.** It runs in fixed `viewBox` units (`420×348`, `quadrant.transform.js:178`) at transform time, and quadrant has no `ResizeObserver`. So box size changes the *scale* and never the *number of labels that fit*.
- **The 59% is shape-invariant** — 59% at landscape, 59% at portrait, 63% at square. The label and `--fs-meta` scale together, so "author it at portrait" is not a workaround.
- **The side void is landscape-only** — 506px of empty band at landscape (685px after #1598), but only 78px at portrait and none at square. A side key rail is nearly free at landscape and is not free at the other two shapes.

## A gap worth closing alongside

**`quadrant.manifest.json` declares neither `capacity` nor `split`.** Roadmap has both (`capacity.hard: 5`, `split.strategy: "roadmap-horizons"`, capped at 4 pages by #1209). Quadrant's density limit exists only as prose in `antiPatterns` — *"Past twelve the labels overlap and the quadrant becomes a constellation"* — so the recommender can't warn, lint can't flag, and the engine can't paginate. Cheap, and it reduces N, which is what unpins the size.

## Done when

Two conditions, deliberately separate — the first is the defect, the second is the goal:

1. **Invariant.** On every quadrant slide in the corpus, at whatever size is chosen, `placeLabels` emits a name for **every** named item — zero `hidden: true` results. Verified by a corpus sweep, not by the two dense fixtures in `quadrant.test.js`, which are a floor and not a ceiling. A regression test pins it so a future size change cannot reintroduce a silent drop.
2. **Target.** Every item name paints at ≥ `--fs-meta`, either beside its dot or in a key rail, on every slide in the corpus. Where a slide falls back to the rail, that is a recorded decision with the threshold stated — not an accident.

Because the choice for the last four slides (key rail vs. split vs. accepting a smaller size there) is a genuine fork with a reader cost, **write the design model before the code** and confirm the direction in one round — per CLAUDE.md § "Design-before-code".
