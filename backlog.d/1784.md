# docs(catalog): `capacity` is absent from 40 of 61 components, and AGENTS.md presents it as the primary authoring defense

## Summary

The per-component budget contract reaches under half the catalog, while `AGENTS.md` instructs every agent to use it as the first step of choosing a component. Measured on `main` at 2026-08-23.

| surface | `capacity` | `density` | both | neither |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `lib/components/**/*.manifest.json` (61) | 19 | 29 | 18 | 31 |
| `dist/docs/components.json` (61) | **21** | 29 | — | — |

Reproduce:

```sh
node -e "
const c=require('./dist/docs/components.json');
const l=Array.isArray(c)?c:(c.components||Object.values(c));
console.log('capacity:',l.filter(x=>x.capacity).length,'density:',l.filter(x=>x.density).length,'of',l.length);
"
```

## Three separate things here

**1. The two surfaces disagree.** `list` and `kpi` carry a `capacity` in the published catalog but carry none in their own manifest — so something in the build is injecting it, and nothing asserts the two agree. That is the shape `checkDensityCoverage` already guards for adaptivity (10 declared vs 25 actual, #—); the same class of drift is unguarded here.

**2. The doc promises a method that is unavailable two thirds of the time.** `AGENTS.md:54` says **"Count first, then filter by capacity"** and sends the reader to the `capacity` column of `components.pick.md`. For 40 of 61 components that column is `—`.

To its credit the pick surface *discloses* this rather than hiding it (`components.pick.md:35`: *"A `—` capacity means no count budget is published for this component — not that it holds unlimited items"*). So this is a coverage gap with honest signposting, not a false claim. The problem is that the instruction upstream in `AGENTS.md` does not carry the same caveat, so an agent following it hits a dead end without being told the method simply does not apply here.

**3. Most of the 31 "neither" are legitimate; 24 are not.** Bookends (`title`/`closing`/`divider`), single-element layouts (`big-number`, `quote`), fence-bodied layouts (`code`, `math`, `diagram`) and media (`image`, `video`, `scene`, `logo-wall`) have no countable collection, and the universal chrome budgets in `lib/authoring/prose-budgets.js` already cover their prose. Excluding those, **24 components do carry a countable collection or a prose body and publish no capacity**:

`content` · `split-panel` · `compare-prose` · `decision` · `split-compare` · `matrix-2x2` · `list-tabular` · `glossary` · `list-criteria` · `obligation-matrix` · `pricing` · `redline` · `timeline-list` · `matrix-grid` · `state-chart` · `journey` · `gantt` · `funnel` · `progress` · `map` · `quadrant` · `radar` · `piechart` · `word-cloud`

`content` is the one I would fix first: it is the catch-all, and it is what an un-classed slide resolves to (#1292), so it is plausibly the most-authored component in the catalog. The chart family (11 of the 24) may warrant a family-level budget rather than 11 individual ones — their axis is data series, not prose items.

## Why it matters

`capacity` is not decoration. It is what `lint:deck` reads to emit `capacity-crowd` / `capacity-overflow`, and — per `components.pick.md:27` — it is *"how you avoid the rework"* rather than discovering the overflow after rendering. Where it is absent, an author or agent gets no pre-render signal at all and falls back on the overflow oracle, which only fires once the deck has been rendered and the content already clipped.

## Swimlane

`engineering/decisions/2026-06-30-prose-density-budget.md` (the budget system), `lib/authoring/prose-budgets.js` (the universal tier that does have full coverage).

## Done when

- [ ] Either every one of the 24 listed components publishes a `capacity`, **or** the ones deliberately exempt say so explicitly in the manifest (an `capacity: null` with a `note`, so "no budget" is a declared decision rather than an omission the gate cannot distinguish from a forgotten field).
- [ ] `AGENTS.md`'s "Count first, then filter by capacity" carries the caveat the pick surface already does, and names what to do when the column reads `—`.
