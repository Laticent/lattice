# fix(a11y): the Mermaid pie legend swatch is never textured — texture makes the chart HARDER to read on the a11y palettes

## Summary

Mermaid's pie legend swatch carries no categorical class, so no `--cat-N-texture` rule reaches it. The wedge gets a texture; its own key stays flat. Measured on rendered pixels under CVD simulation, this makes the chart **worse for the reader the channel exists to serve**.

## Measured — wedge → own-legend-swatch correct matches

| vision | textures off | textures on |
|---|---|---|
| deuteranopia | 11/12 | **3/12** |
| achromatopsia | 8/12 | **1/12** |
| protanopia | 12/12 | 7/12 |
| normal | 12/12 | 10/12 |

The mechanism: the ink overlay drags the wedge's rendered color away from its own flat key. **The drift texture introduces (mean ΔE 0.0184 under deuteranopia) is larger than the entire between-category signal (0.0154).**

`a11y-achromatopsia` is a shipped theme name. On it, a reader can currently match 1 of 12 wedges to its key.

## Compounding: the legend is already unreadable on the a11y palettes

Independently of texture, on `a11y-deuteranopia` the pie legend is 12 near-identical grey squares — adjacent swatch contrast **1.09–1.12:1**. Hue is deliberately gone from that family (it is total greyscale), and nothing replaced it in the key.

So the palette whose entire purpose is hue-free reading ships a key that cannot be read without hue.

## The fix is small and lands whatever else is decided

12 CSS rules pointing the legend swatch at the same `--cat-N-texture` the wedge uses, so key and wedge carry the same tile. Measured at **~1,739 B** uncompressed. This restores key↔wedge correspondence and is a straight repair of a shipped defect on the accessibility palettes.

## Related, same file, same class — texture applied where it does not mean category

`lib/integrations/mermaid/mermaid.css:368-371` pins flowchart node fill by **document order**, so on a textured theme four unrelated process steps come back with three different tile geometries ("Signal Intake" diagonal, "Scoring Model" diagonal, "Decision Log" horizontal rules, "Outcome Store" hatched). Texture is a **nominal** channel; cycling it by position **fabricates categories that do not exist**, and `engineering/textures.md:169-171` already states the channel is not a magnitude channel. This ships today on all 9 textured themes and on the most common Mermaid diagram type.

Worth fixing in the same pass, or splitting out if it grows.

## Also found while measuring — file under the same investigation

- **No test anywhere asserts the 12 tiles are distinguishable.** `test/unit/core/accessibility-textures.test.js` asserts a byte-lock against a golden, an iOS `no var()` guard, and per-family pattern counts. Zero occurrences of `distinct` / `separab` / `discrimin` / the 0.15 ΔE floor in any texture test. `texture-ramp.js`'s `inkContrastRange` explicitly declines to assert a floor.
- Effective as-painted stroke-vs-fill contrast measures **1.26:1 on onyx** (min 1.03) and **1.35:1** on a derived brand theme, against the **3:1** non-text floor the repo enforces on every other diagram graphic. The texture ink escapes that gate only because the gate enumerates stroke tokens.
- Tile ink coverage ranges **9.3% – 50.0%** (5.4x), laying an apparent *ordinal* ramp over a nominal channel — the same principle violation as the flowchart item.
- `engineering/textures.md:110` claims "the canonical rules (mermaid.css, the pie block, **the chart family**) already paint `var(--cat-N-texture, …)`". The chart-family half is **false** — Lattice's native SVG charts consume no `--cat-N-texture`; `themes/a11y-base.css:279-298` hand-wires 18 rules straight at `url(#latt-a11y-chart-tex-N)`. The doc contradicts itself at :171.
