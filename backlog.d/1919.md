# quadrant: two charts on adjacent slides render at different sizes, because a lede taxes the stage

## Summary

Two quadrant slides side by side render at visibly different sizes. The difference is entirely how much prose the author put above the chart — not the data, not the variant.

## Swimlane

`engineering/decisions/2026-08-12-chart-body-block-padding.md` (#1631, merged) — this is the half that record explicitly leaves open. Adjacent to #1605, which needs the same lever for a different reason (see below).

## Done when

Two quadrant slides carrying identical data and differing only in masthead content (a `.chart-subtitle` present vs absent) render their charts at the same size, verified by measuring the `<svg>` border box on the real render at landscape, portrait and square. The corpus spread across landscape quadrant slides is stated in the PR, before and after.

---

## Reported

Observed in the docs Playground: the `default` quadrant (slide 2) is noticeably smaller than `bubble` (slide 3). Real, and #1631 did **not** fix it — that change made every chart bigger, which is a different complaint.

## Root cause — measured, settled, don't re-derive

The chart is **height-bound**, so its paint scale is `stageHeight ÷ vbHeight`, and stage height is whatever the masthead leaves. A `.chart-subtitle` is **36.3px**, and the stage absorbs exactly that. Because the paint is height-bound, a height loss is also a **width** loss — the whole chart shrinks on both axes.

Measured on `quadrant.gallery.md` at landscape, pre-#1631:

| slide | lede | masthead | stage | `<svg>` box |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 `default` | yes | 124.7 | 387.3 | 1152×**323.3** |
| 3 `bubble` | no | 88.3 | 423.7 | 1152×**359.7** |

The height budget accounts for it exactly — `423.7 − 387.3 = 36.4` = the lede:

```
section                720.0
  − padding-block      192.0   (88 top / 104 bottom)
  − masthead            88.3   (eyebrow 18.6 + h2 44.8 + 24 pad)  [+36.3 with a lede]
  − row gap             16.0
  = .cell-stage        423.7
```

**Corpus spread across 17 landscape quadrant slides: 319.3 → 378.2px (18.4%).** Other levers on the same mechanism: a heading that wraps (~45px), the `compact` / `accent` finishes.

**Post-#1631, on the live Playground at 390px: 96 vs 105px (9.4%).** Improved only because the fixed 36.3px is now a smaller fraction of a bigger stage — the mechanism is untouched.

## The fork

- **Stabilize the stage.** Make a `chart-frame` stage deterministic so masthead content stops taxing the chart. Pure CSS, kills the spread outright. **But it levels DOWN**: roomy slides lose ~10% to match tight ones, which partly gives back what #1631 just won.
- **Absorb it into the void.** Let the chart keep its size and spend the variance on the ~60% of width it currently discards. Strictly better for the reader, much larger, and needs the adaptive-unit work (`2026-08-10-quadrant-unit-aspect.md` §2–3; see #1605).

**Coupling worth knowing before choosing:** #1605's acceptance check #2 is *not satisfiable* while this variance exists, because the legibility bar is `--fs-meta ÷ paint scale` and therefore moves with the masthead (measured range 13.3 → 18.1 units across the corpus). Option 1 makes that bar a single number and unblocks #1605's design; option 2 does not. That may matter more than the ~10% either way.
