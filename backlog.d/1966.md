# spike(exemplars): the style judges are calibrated on a corpus nobody has validated — break the circularity before deriving more

## Summary

Establish, independently of the scorer, whether `exemplars/` is actually "what good looks like" — because the style judges were calibrated **from** those decks, so using the scorer to grade them is circular.

## Swimlane

`engineering/decisions/2026-08-25-deck-profiles-craft-style-split.md` — the record that built the Style half and the three profiles, and whose §3 derived the per-family word/heading budgets from these very decks. Follow-on to `engineering/decisions/2026-08-30-craft-weight-variance-proxy-bias.md`, which established the general failure mode: *a measurement made on a corpus is evidence about that corpus, not about the thing you wanted to price.*

## The problem

`exemplars/README.md` calls the library "what good looks like", and the Studio's Drafting picker serves those decks live — so they are simultaneously (a) the calibration basis for the Style budgets and (b) what users copy. That makes their quality load-bearing twice over, and nobody has independently established it.

Measured just now with the merged scorer over all 45 exemplars:

| family | n | mean Craft | mean Style |
|---|---|---|---|
| corporate | 9 | 100.0 | 89.1 |
| general-team | 11 | 100.0 | 82.6 |
| academic | 10 | 100.0 | 82.5 |
| government-public | 8 | 99.5 | 82.5 |
| nonprofit | 7 | 100.0 | **79.7** |
| **all** | **45** | **99.9** | **83.4** |

**40 of 45 (89%) score below 93 on at least one half.** The worst are `impact-annual-report.md` (Style 67, C+) and `compliance-audit-report.md` (Style 70, B). The library that teaches good averages **B+** against the repo's own scorer.

Three findings sharpen it:

1. **Craft is pinned at ~100 and discriminates nothing here** (44 of 45 are exactly 100). That is the same flatness artifact `contract` and `pacing` turned out to have — the exemplars are lint-clean by the same gate, so Craft cannot see anything.
2. **Zero of the 45 declare a `profile:`.** All are judged against `general`. The `mission` profile — built and calibrated *specifically* on the nonprofit family's measured heading lengths — is used by **none** of the nonprofit exemplars, which are the worst-scoring family. `teaching` likewise governs only its two declared decks.
3. **`framing` is the dominant deduction** (`52` = both `no-ask` and `agenda-missing` firing), on decks where it is plausibly genre-correct: a `literature-review` or a `lecture` may legitimately have no ask.

## The circularity, which is the actual point

The scorer cannot answer whether these decks are good, because §3 of the governing record derived the budgets **from these decks' own percentiles**. Grading them with it asks the corpus whether it resembles itself. The B+ average is therefore consistent with either reading, and the scorer cannot separate them:

- the exemplars really are mediocre and the budgets inherited that mediocrity; **or**
- they are fine, and the deductions are the profile-fit failure of finding (2) — 45 genre decks judged against `general` because none declares what it is.

Most likely both, in some proportion nothing currently measures. Deriving *more* judges from this corpus before settling it would compound the error rather than fix it, which is why this is a spike and not an implementation card.

## Done when

A dated decision doc under `engineering/decisions/` that:

1. **Separates the two readings with a measurement, not an opinion.** Concretely: re-score all 45 with each family assigned its best-fitting declared profile, and report how much of the 83.4 mean Style gap is profile-fit versus genuine deduction. `npm run score:variance` and `scoreDeck` already do the work; the missing input is the family→profile assignment.
2. **States an independent quality bar** — how we judge an exemplar good *without* the scorer whose budgets came from it (the boardroom 10/10 rubric in `engineering/decisions/2026-06-06-layout-audit/` is the obvious candidate, applied to rendered PDFs, per the QUALITY BAR's visual-review path).
3. **Applies that bar to a named sample** — not all 45; enough to say whether the corpus needs remediation, with the worst two (`impact-annual-report`, `compliance-audit-report`) and the best two included.
4. **Recommends, with its cost:** remediate the corpus, declare profiles on it, both, or neither.
5. Answers explicitly: **does deriving style judges from exemplars remain a good idea after (1)–(4)?** A "no" is a legitimate and useful outcome.

Out of scope: rewriting any exemplar deck, changing any budget, weight, or rule, and building any new judge. Each of those is a separate card that this spike either justifies or kills.

## Why this is worth doing

Every future style judge inherits this corpus's blind spots, and the Drafting picker propagates them to users' own decks. If the exemplars are 10/10 the spike is cheap and we proceed with confidence; if they are not, we found it before building on top of it — which is exactly the failure the 2026-08-30 record documents one level down.
