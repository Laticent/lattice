# [Idea] Chat Tools and Capabilities

## Summary

While splitting and polishing a 36-slide deck in Studio, the Architect (chat assistant) kept hitting the same gaps. It couldn't edit nested front matter, so pronunciation fixes needed hand-pasted YAML. It couldn't move or copy slides between decks, so a deck split meant retyping. It couldn't preview how text will be spoken, so it guessed. This ticket collects those gaps as 22 proposed actions, each with context, a proposed behavior and acceptance criteria. They are grouped by priority.

## Background: the session that surfaced these

- **Goal:** split a long deck into smaller ones (main argument, ratings/heatmap, shoes and patterns), then improve text-to-speech (TTS) pronunciation.
- **Pronunciation fixes:** "Twinkima.G" became "Twinkee mah Gee", which worked after the author pasted the line. "Derby" became "Darby", which also had to be pasted by hand. The assistant couldn't write either entry.
- **Find and replace:** "Oct. Tenth" to "October Tenth" was done by reading slides and editing each one.
- **Narrative audit:** the assistant found Yearn-directed asks (lining palette, lead times, patina) placed before the "what I'd like next" section. It found them by reading slides one at a time.
- **State mismatch:** the assistant's earlier replies described edits (a divider at slide 47, title-style dividers at slide 36) that the later deck view did not contain. The deck then showed 35 or 36 slides. The author had no reliable way to tell whether an edit had landed.
- **New deck:** the assistant could submit a whole new deck as one finished Markdown draft. It couldn't duplicate this deck, move slides across, or build the new deck up over several turns.

## P1: highest impact

### 1. Edit nested front-matter maps
- **Problem:** the front-matter tool accepts one key and one single-line value. Maps such as `acronyms:`, `lexicon:`, `say:`, `finish-override:` and `progress-rail` options can't be edited.
- **Evidence:** every pronunciation change (Twinkima.G, Derby with four case and plural variants, the 19-entry list) had the author pasting YAML by hand.
- **Proposal:** an action that adds, updates or removes one entry in a named map, for example `acronyms["Derby"] = "Darby"`. It should support several entries per call and return the resulting map.
- **Acceptance:**
  - Existing entries are preserved.
  - Duplicate keys are reported.
  - Quoting and escaping are handled, including periods, apostrophes and curly quotes.
  - The change appears on the diff card like any other edit.

### 2. Move and copy slides, within a deck or into a new deck
- **Problem:** to reorder slides or carry them into another deck, the assistant must retype each slide's Markdown. That risks wording drift, loses notes or spoken lines if it misses them, and costs a lot of output.
- **Evidence:** the requested split into separate decks needed the author to copy and paste by hand. The assistant could only draft a new deck from scratch.
- **Proposal:** actions to move slides (range to position), copy slides (range to position or to a new deck), and create a new deck from a slide range. The new deck should inherit front matter (theme, finish, typography, acronyms) by default, with an option to add a title and closing slide.
- **Acceptance:**
  - Slide content, notes and spoken lines are carried over byte-for-byte.
  - The author confirms the source and target before anything is applied.
  - There's an option to remove the copied slides from the source.

### 3. Spoken-text preview
- **Problem:** the assistant can't hear TTS output. It guesses at pronunciations, and the author tests them by playing slides.
- **Evidence:** guesses for "Ilcea" and "Sharmarke" were flagged as uncertain. The Twinkima.G and Derby fixes were found by trial. It was also unclear whether possessives such as "Twinkima.G's" match the acronym entry.
- **Proposal:** an action that returns the exact text the voice will read for a slide or range, after applying the acronym list, lexicon, `say:` lines and slide notes. It should mark which substitutions fired and which terms had no match.
- **Acceptance:**
  - It covers titles, body, notes and spoken lines.
  - It flags unmatched tokens that look hard to pronounce, such as unusual capitalization, periods inside words and non-English names.
  - Possessives and plurals are reported as separate matches.

### 4. Create and manage reader lenses
- **Problem:** a lens is an approved subset of slides, shown to a different audience without editing the deck. The assistant knows how lenses work but can't define one.
- **Evidence:** the author's split could have been two lenses over one deck, "main argument" and "shoes and patterns", with no copying and no duplicated content to maintain.
- **Proposal:** actions to create, rename, edit and delete a lens. Slides are chosen by number or range and kept in a stable order. The assistant can preview the lens's slide list.
- **Acceptance:**
  - Lens membership survives slide reordering.
  - The lens shows its slide count and estimated talk time.
  - It warns about orphaned references, for example a slide that points to a removed section.

### 5. Edit status and sync visibility
- **Problem:** the assistant has no reliable signal for whether a staged edit was applied, rejected or still pending. It told the author about changes the later deck view didn't contain.
- **Evidence:** earlier replies described a divider at slide 47 and title-style dividers at slide 36, but the deck the assistant saw later had 35 or 36 slides and neither.
- **Proposal:** after each turn, tell the assistant the status of the previous turn's edits (applied, rejected, partly applied or superseded), and give it a deck revision or hash it can cite.
- **Acceptance:**
  - The assistant can say "your edit applied" or "didn't apply" with certainty.
  - The deck brief shows the revision.
  - A mismatch between what the assistant believes and the actual deck is surfaced to the author as a warning.

## P2: strong productivity gains

### 6. Find and replace across the deck, with preview
- **Problem:** terms are replaced by reading slide after slide. It's easy to miss instances in notes, spoken lines, image alt text, eyebrows and quote attributions.
- **Evidence:** "Oct. Tenth" appeared in titles, eyebrows, body text, attributions and spoken lines across slides 11–16 and 28.
- **Proposal:** search across all text fields, with a match list (slide, field, context) and optional replace-all or replace-selected. It should support case-sensitive, whole-word and regex options.
- **Acceptance:**
  - Matches inside front matter are listed separately.
  - The replace appears as one diff card.
  - The count of replaced instances is reported.

### 7. Whole-deck outline and argument view
- **Problem:** to judge flow, the assistant reads slides one by one, and the deck outline doesn't include notes or placement of asks.
- **Evidence:** the audit that found out-of-place requests (lining palette on slide 14, lead-time advice on slide 16, patina on slide 28) took many reads.
- **Proposal:** one call returns every heading, layout, word count, note length, section boundary and any flags: duplicate claims, headings that start alike, and asks or recommendations appearing before the "ask" section.
- **Acceptance:**
  - The output fits one response for a 50-slide deck.
  - Section boundaries come from dividers.
  - The view supports a "headings only" mode that reads as the argument.

### 8. Talk-time estimate
- **Problem:** the assistant can only guess how long the deck runs against a time slot.
- **Evidence:** 36 slides with roughly 1,800 words is likely over a typical slot, but nobody measured it.
- **Proposal:** estimate minutes per slide and in total from spoken lines, notes or on-screen text, using the deck's pace setting. Compare against a target length the author gives.
- **Acceptance:**
  - It lists the slowest slides.
  - It gives a total.
  - It suggests which slides to cut or move to a lens if over target.

### 9. Multi-slide render and contact sheet
- **Problem:** rendering is limited to three single slides per turn, which isn't enough to review the look of a long deck.
- **Proposal:** render a grid of up to 12 thumbnails per call, plus a full-size render on request. Flag thumbnails that overflow or look inconsistent.
- **Acceptance:** one call gives a contact sheet for a range, with slide numbers visible.

### 10. Build a new deck across turns
- **Problem:** a new deck is one submission per turn, accepted or rejected. It can't be built up or revised in place.
- **Proposal:** a draft-deck workspace the assistant and author both see. The assistant can add, replace or remove slides in it over several turns, and the author confirms once at the end.
- **Acceptance:**
  - The draft survives across turns.
  - It shows an outline and a diff since the last turn.
  - Confirming creates the deck. Discarding changes nothing.

### 11. Export, share and validate
- **Problem:** the assistant can't run PDF, offline player or share-link exports, and so can't tell the author whether a fix survived export.
- **Proposal:** actions to run an export and report results (fonts embedded, images optimized, overflow in print, broken links), and to create a share link with a chosen scope such as a lens.
- **Acceptance:**
  - The export reports failures per slide.
  - The assistant can retry after a fix.
  - Sharing always needs author confirmation.

## P3: valuable additions

### 12. Accessibility audit with fixes
- **Problem:** alt text and contrast are reviewed by reading, which is unreliable.
- **Proposal:** list every image without alt text, every low-contrast pairing, color-only signals and type below the legibility floor, with a suggested fix for each.
- **Acceptance:** results are per slide, with a one-click fix path that goes through the normal diff card.

### 13. Compare two decks or two versions
- **Problem:** there's no way to see what the new lookbook shares with the original, or what changed since an earlier session.
- **Proposal:** a slide-level and word-level comparison between any two decks or saved versions.
- **Acceptance:** it reports added, removed, moved and changed slides, and can compare against a lens.

### 14. Version history and undo
- **Problem:** the assistant can't compare before and after across a session, or revert part of it.
- **Proposal:** named checkpoints, plus the ability to revert one slide, one key or a whole turn.
- **Acceptance:** each applied diff card creates a checkpoint, and a revert is itself a reviewable diff.

### 15. Image handling
- **Problem:** the assistant can only use photos already in the library. It can't crop, reorder collages by content, or accept a photo sent in chat.
- **Proposal:** upload from chat, crop or focus-point setting, and auto-suggest alt text from the caption it saves.
- **Acceptance:** uploads land in the library with a caption, and image edits are non-destructive.

### 16. Data import from files
- **Problem:** the chart tool takes rows the assistant types, which risks transcription errors.
- **Proposal:** read a CSV or spreadsheet from the library, preview the columns, and build the chart or table from the selected ones.
- **Acceptance:** units are preserved, and the source file is referenced in the notes.

### 17. Translate a deck
- **Problem:** translating means retyping slides, notes, spoken lines and the pronunciation list by hand.
- **Proposal:** translate a deck or a range into a chosen language, set the language keys, and offer a localized acronym list. Keep layouts and word budgets intact.
- **Acceptance:** the result is a new deck by default, and the tool flags places where the translation is longer than the budget.

## New ideas

### 18. Batch spoken-line and notes generation
- **Problem:** many slides read straight from on-screen text, and spoken lines are written slide by slide.
- **Proposal:** draft spoken lines or notes for a range in one pass, in the author's voice. It should use existing lines as style examples and apply the pronunciation list.
- **Acceptance:** it skips slides that already have a line unless asked, and shows the results on one diff card.

### 19. Ask-placement and narrative lint
- **Problem:** requests and recommendations show up too early, and some top-five items are never argued earlier in the deck.
- **Evidence:** the lining palette, lead times and patina cases, plus a top-five list item with no supporting slide.
- **Proposal:** a deck check that traces each recommendation in the closing slides back to the slide that argues for it. It flags asks in praise sections and unsupported asks near the close.
- **Acceptance:** output lists each ask, where it is argued, and where it first appears.

### 20. Pronunciation helper
- **Problem:** the author and assistant iterate by guessing respellings and replaying slides.
- **Proposal:** scan for terms likely to trip a voice (names, initialisms, periods inside words, mixed case). Propose two or three respellings each, and keep a deck-level "confirmed by author" flag so guesses aren't repeated.
- **Acceptance:** each term shows status (confirmed, guessed, untested) and the slides it appears in.

### 21. Partial apply on the diff card
- **Problem:** the author applies or rejects the whole turn at once.
- **Proposal:** let the author accept individual slide edits within a card.
- **Acceptance:** rejected parts don't block accepted ones, and the assistant is told which parts landed.

### 22. Duplicate, rename and delete decks
- **Problem:** the assistant can't duplicate this deck as a starting point for a split, or tidy up experiments.
- **Proposal:** duplicate, rename and archive actions, each needing author confirmation.
- **Acceptance:** duplicates keep front matter, assets and lenses, and deletion is reversible for a set time.

### 23. Anonymous bug reports and feature requests via GitHub
- **Problem:** the assistant hits product gaps and bugs during a session but can only describe them in chat. The author has to carry them to the team by hand, and many are never filed.
- **Evidence:** this session produced 22 tool gaps and one state mismatch, all reported only in conversation.
- **Proposal:** an action that drafts a bug report or feature request from the session, shows the author the exact text, and on confirmation files it as a GitHub issue through a project relay account. No author name, email or workspace identifier is sent. Deck content is excluded unless the author attaches a specific excerpt.
- **Acceptance:**
  - The author sees and can edit the full payload before sending.
  - Separate bug and feature templates are used, with a `from-architect` label.
  - The assistant searches open issues first and proposes a +1 comment on a duplicate.
  - The relay is rate-limited and rejects empty or repeated submissions.
  - Declining still yields a copy-ready report.
- **Open questions:** who owns the relay account? Is a bug report's Studio version and layout name safe to send, and does it count as identifying? Should reports be public, or go to a private triage repository first?

### 24. Word budget analysis for a slide, a component or the whole deck
- **Problem:** the assistant sees a total per slide and a short list of over-budget slides. It can't see how a slide's words are spread across its parts, or how each part compares with its layout's own limit. Trimming means reading a slide and guessing where the weight is.
- **Evidence:** in a 36-slide deck (1,813 words), four slides were over the 70-word budget (84, 73, 73 and 72 words) and eight more had a single item running past its layout's soft target. Fixing them needed slide-by-slide reading.
- **Proposal:** an analysis action with three scopes.
  - **Slide or component:** words per element (heading, subtitle, eyebrow, each card title and body, key insight, notes, spoken line) against that layout's limits, with the overage per element.
  - **Section:** totals and averages by divider-bounded section, with the heaviest slides ranked.
  - **Deck:** total and per-slide counts, the distribution (median, longest, how many are over), and a comparison with the deck's profile budget and with other profiles.
  - **Fix guidance:** for each over-budget element, the number of words to cut and the likely fix: tighten, move to notes, or split the slide.
- **Acceptance:**
  - Counts match what the deck check reports.
  - Limits come from each layout's own budget, not only the global 70.
  - Output is per slide and per element, and fits one response for a 50-slide deck.
  - Notes and spoken-line words are reported separately from on-screen words.
  - Any suggested trim goes through the normal diff card and is never applied automatically.
- **Open questions:** should markup (bold, pills, code spans) count as words? How are numbers and symbols such as "10–14" counted?

### 25. Token-efficient editing and retrieval tools
- **Problem:** the assistant resends whole slides to change a few words, reads many slides to find one term, regenerates long documents on request, and retypes slides to carry them between decks. This raises cost and latency and increases the chance of wording drift.
- **Evidence:** in one session, a single term replacement meant resending about seven full slides. A narrative audit needed slide-by-slide reads. A 22-item ticket was output in full three times.
- **Proposal:**
  - **Patch edits:** replace a string, set a heading, set or append a note or spoken line, or change one element, addressed by slide and field.
  - **Search with snippets:** return slide, field and context for each match, so only hits are read.
  - **Move and copy by reference:** the Studio carries the content; the assistant sends only slide numbers.
  - **Saved documents:** the assistant writes a document once, edits sections of it later and can show it again without regenerating it.
  - **Compact reads:** headings only, notes only, spoken lines only, or one element type for a range.
  - **Range operations:** apply a modifier, tone or note template across a set of slides in one step.
  - **Computed lint and outline:** word counts per element, repeated openings and misplaced asks, computed by the Studio with a compact mode.
  - **Session summary:** a short, Studio-maintained list of decisions and confirmed facts that replaces replaying the full history.
- **Acceptance:**
  - A one-word change costs output proportional to the change, not the slide.
  - Patch edits still appear on the normal diff card.
  - Search results cover titles, body, notes, spoken lines, alt text and front matter.
  - Compact modes return under a set size for a 50-slide deck.
  - Copy and move preserve content, notes and spoken lines exactly.
- **Open questions:** what's the right granularity for field addressing? Should a session summary be visible to and editable by the author? How should the Studio handle a patch whose target text has changed since it was read?


## Trust, memory, safety and quality

### 26. Accurate self-description of tools and limits
- **Problem:** the assistant describes its own abilities from memory and can under- or over-claim. The author then plans around the wrong picture.
- **Evidence:** in one session the assistant first said it could only describe a new deck, then showed it could submit one. It also described edits that the later deck view did not contain.
- **Proposal:** at the start of each session, give the assistant a generated list of its current tools, their limits (for example "front matter: single-line values only") and what each one can change. Update it whenever tools change.
- **Acceptance:**
  - The list is generated from the real tool definitions, not hand-written.
  - Answers about capabilities cite that list.
  - Limits are stated per tool, including what is not supported.
- **Open questions:** should the author be able to see the same list in the Studio?

### 27. Read-back after every applied edit
- **Problem:** after the author applies a diff card, the assistant has no confirmation of what the deck now contains. It can't tell whether an edit landed as intended.
- **Evidence:** earlier replies described a divider at slide 47 and title-style dividers at slide 36, but a later view of the deck showed neither.
- **Proposal:** after an edit is applied, return a short verification for each touched slide: the heading, the layout, the word count and the first and last lines. Report any slide that differs from what was staged.
- **Acceptance:**
  - The read-back arrives automatically on the next turn.
  - It reports applied, rejected, partly applied or superseded for each edit.
  - A mismatch is shown to the author as a warning.
- **Open questions:** how much detail is enough without adding to token use? This pairs with #5 (edit status and sync visibility).

### 28. Structured failure reasons and alternatives
- **Problem:** when a tool refuses or can't do something, the assistant gets little to go on. It can say "I can't", but not always why or what to do instead.
- **Evidence:** the front-matter tool couldn't take the nested `acronyms:` block, so the author had to paste YAML. The assistant had to work out the cause and the workaround on its own.
- **Proposal:** every refusal or error returns a code, a plain-language reason and one or more suggested alternatives, for example "nested values are not supported; paste this block into the front matter yourself".
- **Acceptance:**
  - Each error has a stable code and a human-readable reason.
  - Alternatives are listed where they exist.
  - The assistant can pass the reason on to the author without guessing.
- **Open questions:** who maintains the list of alternatives as tools change?

### 29. Remembered author decisions
- **Problem:** facts the author confirms are lost between sessions. A new session may ask again or repeat a guess the author already rejected.
- **Evidence:** "Twinkima.G" as "Twinkee mah Gee" and "Derby" as "Darby" were confirmed by the author. Earlier guesses for "Ilcea" and "Sharmarke" were never confirmed and could be repeated.
- **Proposal:** a deck-level notes area where confirmed decisions are stored, such as pronunciations, naming choices and structure rules like "no asks before the final section". The assistant reads it at the start of each session and can add to it with the author's approval.
- **Acceptance:**
  - Each entry shows its status: confirmed by the author, or guessed.
  - The author can view, edit and delete entries.
  - Entries travel with the deck, including when it is copied into a new deck.
- **Open questions:** should this live in front matter, in a separate file, or in the Studio?

### 30. House style rules enforced by the deck check
- **Problem:** style rules are held in the assistant's memory and in the author's head. Nothing enforces them across edits or sessions.
- **Evidence:** the asks that appeared too early in the praise sections (the lining palette, lead times and patina) broke a rule that was only stated in conversation.
- **Proposal:** a short, deck-level list of rules the author defines, such as US spelling, banned words, maximum title length and where asks may appear. The deck check evaluates them and reports violations per slide.
- **Acceptance:**
  - Rules are plain text or simple options, not code.
  - Violations name the slide, the element and the rule.
  - Rules apply to the assistant's own edits before they are staged.
- **Open questions:** which rules can be checked reliably by a program, and which need a judgment call by the assistant?

### 31. Audit log of assistant actions
- **Problem:** the author can't see a single list of what the assistant changed, searched or sent. This matters more as the assistant gains actions with wider reach.
- **Proposal:** a reviewable log of every assistant action: reads, staged edits, applied edits, new decks, library captions, exports and any outbound report. Each entry shows time, target and outcome.
- **Acceptance:**
  - The log is visible to the author and can be exported.
  - Staged and applied changes are linked to their diff cards.
  - Any action that leaves the workspace is clearly marked.
- **Open questions:** how long is the log kept, and who else can see it?

### 32. Privacy boundaries made visible
- **Problem:** it isn't clear to the author which data can leave the workspace, such as deck text, photos, captions and notes. That needs to be settled before outbound features exist.
- **Evidence:** the proposed anonymous GitHub reporting (#23), exports and share links would each send data outside the Studio.
- **Proposal:** a clear, author-visible policy and setting for what the assistant may send out, with deck content excluded by default. Each outbound action shows the exact payload before it is sent.
- **Acceptance:**
  - Defaults exclude slide text, notes, photos and captions.
  - Including an excerpt is opt-in and shown in a preview.
  - The author can see which actions are able to send data out.
- **Open questions:** does the Studio version or a layout name count as identifying information?

### 33. Prompt-injection test set
- **Problem:** deck text, photo captions and any text inside images are treated as data, but this isn't verified as new tools are added. A hostile caption or slide could try to trigger an action.
- **Proposal:** a maintained set of hostile test content, such as slide text, captions, alt text and text inside photos that instruct the assistant to edit, send or share. Run it against every new tool before release.
- **Acceptance:**
  - No test causes an action the author did not ask for.
  - Tools that send data outward are tested first.
  - Results are recorded for each release.
- **Open questions:** who owns the test set, and where does it live?

### 34. Evaluation suite built from real sessions
- **Problem:** there is no way to tell whether a Studio release made the assistant better or worse at real work.
- **Evidence:** this session covered a deck split, pronunciation fixes, a find-and-replace, a narrative audit and a new-deck draft. Each is a repeatable task.
- **Proposal:** a set of replayable scenarios drawn from real sessions, with checks for outcome, accuracy of claims and effort. Run it for every release and compare with the last.
- **Acceptance:**
  - Each scenario has a clear pass or fail check.
  - Claims about the deck are compared with its actual state.
  - Results show the change since the previous release.
- **Open questions:** how are real sessions anonymized before they become tests?

### 35. Usage visibility for the author
- **Problem:** the author can't see how much each turn cost or where the effort went. That makes it hard to judge whether the token-saving tools (#25) are worth building first.
- **Proposal:** show a rough per-turn summary: input read, output written, tools called and time taken. Provide a session total.
- **Acceptance:**
  - The summary appears on each turn, collapsed by default.
  - It separates reading, writing and tool calls.
  - Totals are available per deck and per session.
- **Open questions:** should this be shown to every author, or only to an admin or the team?

### 36. Progress and cancel for long jobs
- **Problem:** long operations, such as a deck-wide audit, a multi-slide render or an export, are all-or-nothing. The author can't see progress or stop one.
- **Proposal:** show progress with a step count for any job that takes more than a few seconds, with partial results as they arrive and a cancel control.
- **Acceptance:**
  - Cancelling leaves the deck unchanged.
  - Partial results are kept and labeled as partial.
  - The assistant is told whether a job finished, was canceled or failed.
- **Open questions:** which operations need this first? Exports and deck-wide audits are the likely candidates.

### 37. Rehearsal support
- **Problem:** the author can't tell where a talk runs long until they present it. Word counts and the talk-time estimate (#8) are only predictions.
- **Evidence:** the deck has 36 slides and roughly 1,800 words, which is likely over a typical slot, but nobody has measured it.
- **Proposal:** a timed run-through mode that records time per slide, compares it with the estimate and the target length, and reports where the author ran long or short. The assistant can then suggest cuts or a lens (#4) for a shorter version.
- **Acceptance:**
  - Per-slide timings are saved and comparable across runs.
  - Slides that ran well over estimate are listed.
  - Suggested cuts go through the normal diff card.
- **Open questions:** is the timing taken from the author's real pace, or from the spoken-line estimate?
