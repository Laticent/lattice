# Ask the browser to keep a person’s work (persistent storage)

## Summary

Ask the browser to keep a person's work: request persistent storage when they first save something of their own, and tell them when the browser refuses.

## Swimlane

#3525 (Studio boot and storage). This piece is split out because it is a data-loss risk today and does not wait for the redesign.

### Why

Nothing in `docs/src` calls `navigator.storage.persist()`. Without it, browsers treat the site's storage as disposable. Safari in particular can delete it under storage pressure, or after about seven days without a visit, and that includes a person's stored decks, photos (IndexedDB `lattice-workbench`), chats and checkpoints. A person loses work they never exported, with no warning.

## Done when

1. The Studio calls `navigator.storage.persist()` once, at the first moment a person saves something of their own (a new or edited deck, an added photo), never on first page load before they have anything to keep.
2. It reads `navigator.storage.persisted()` and, when the browser said no, shows the person once, in plain words, that this browser may clear their work, with a one-tap way to export or back up (the existing workspace backup).
3. Measured on the real surfaces (HARD RULE #23): what `persist()` returns, and whether a prompt appears, on iPhone Safari (iOS Simulator suite), desktop Safari, Chrome and Firefox. Recorded in the PR.
4. A test for the decision logic (when to ask, when to show the notice, never twice) with a failing arm (HARD RULE #34).
5. Docs and a changeset in the same change (HARD RULE #10).
