# Lente: the deck state machine — outline, sections, slides, panes, pages and views

## Summary

Grow **Lente** from reader lenses into **the deck's state machine**: the one owner of a deck's outline, sections, slides, panes and pages, and of every view cut from them. Fast enough to run on every keystroke, general purpose, usable in other domains, with Lattice as its first user. It serves every persona: authors, presenters, the people in the room and the people who are not.

## Swimlane

It extends `engineering/decisions/2026-07-13-lente-reader-lenses.md` and absorbs two follow-ups: `followups.d/3287-p1-lente-slide-spine.md` and `followups.d/3456-p2-lente-outline-and-views.md`.

### Owner direction

Three rulings, newest last:

- **2026-11-17**: one slide spine, preferably in Lente, owning slide boundaries, ids, order, on-screen splitting, hidden slides and lens membership; making every structural edit; handing out views to the Studio, the CLI export and the chat alike, as the deck's one state machine.
- **2026-11-23**: "lente will become more than it is today including being able to host outline and slice and dice a deck into views logically and physically (we have had trouble with this and the export)".
- **2026-11-25**: Lente becomes **the state machine for the deck**. It has the outline; it knows slides and panes; it has page information and section information; it has to be **super fast**; it uses Segno and the other libraries where it needs them; it is **general purpose and usable in other domains**, with Lattice the first user. It keeps doing what it does today (slicing together viewers), becomes the **backbone for outlines and tables of contents**, and can slice together **not just slides but panes** to create a new deck view. It can **hand over the deck with its view information, or materialize one view** so the receiver gets only that view. It serves **every persona**.

### What Lente owns

**The model.** One structure, from the top down:

| Level | What it is | Lattice today |
|---|---|---|
| Deck | the whole document | the Markdown file |
| Section | a run of slides under one heading | not a first-class thing yet |
| Slide | one logical slide, with a stable id | cut by about a dozen separate functions that disagree on numbering |
| Pane | one part of a slide's body (two components on one slide) | `lib/core/panes.js`, `                ` |
| Page | one physical page a slide becomes when it is too long for one (`split:`, the measured split) | counted separately by the editor and the export: the export trouble |

**The outline.** Ids, order, titles and nesting: the backbone for the Studio's outline, a table of contents, navigation, and any reader that needs to know where it is.

**Structural edits.** Insert, delete, move, duplicate, at every level, as one atomic operation that keeps ids and every index-keyed fact in step.

**Views.** A view is a selection from the model:
- **Logical:** which units a reader sees. Today's lenses, plus ranges, sections, persona views and the chat's read.
- **Physical:** how the chosen units become pages.
- **Composed:** a new deck view built from slides **and panes**, for example one pane from each of three slides arranged as a new slide, without copying or editing the source.

**Two ways out:**
1. **The deck with its views:** the full model plus view definitions, for a receiver that switches views itself (the Studio, the HTML player).
2. **A materialized view:** only that view's content, nothing else. For a reader who should not receive the rest, such as an exported handout or a deck sent outside the company.

### Personas

| Persona | What Lente gives them |
|---|---|
| **Authors** | the outline and table of contents, structural edits, approving each view before anyone reads it (the human-in-the-loop guarantee of today's lenses stays) |
| **Presenters** | the presenting view, their notes, where they are in the outline, the page each slide became |
| **People in the room** | the view on the screen, kept in step with the presenter |
| **People not in the room** | a view of their own: the deck with views to explore, or a materialized view that holds only what they were given |

### Performance

**Super fast is a requirement.** The model updates incrementally on each keystroke, never by re-reading the whole deck. Parsing goes through Segno, whose grammars read in time proportional to their input, so a hostile or huge deck cannot make it hang. The design note sets measured budgets (an edit, a view projection, a materialization, on a small and a very large deck), and a bench scenario holds them (HARD RULE #19).

### General purpose

Lente's core knows **documents of nested units and the views cut from them**. It does not know slides, Markdown or Lattice. Lattice plugs in through an adapter that says what a deck's sections, slides, panes and pages are. It stays framework-free, has no DOM, and depends only on our own general-purpose libraries (Segno and whichever others it needs), as it does today. Another domain (a book's chapters, a report's sections, a course's lessons) plugs in the same way.

### Questions the design note settles with the owner

1. **Where it lives.** The CLI export needs it too (HARD RULE #1), so its core likely moves from `docs/src/lib/lente/` to `lib/`.
2. **Ids.** Written into the file, or kept in memory for the session (the 2026-11-17 note recommends memory, with every per-slide fact stored inside its slide).
3. **Who owns pages.** A physical view owns page splitting, so the editor and the export stop counting on their own. This includes the measured split from #2988.
4. **Pane-level views.** How a composed view names and approves a pane taken from one slide into another, and what happens when the source pane changes.
5. **Approval survives change.** How today's approval hashes on lenses carry over to sections, panes and composed views.
6. **Persona views.** Which views are built in, and which are authored.
