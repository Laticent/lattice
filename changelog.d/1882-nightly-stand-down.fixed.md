- Every nightly watch can now **act on a recovery** instead of only ever filing. Five of the six could open a rolling issue and none could close one, so a thread's first firing — likelier a harness failure than a real regression — turned it into "the flaky nightly", and a genuine regression months later arrived as a comment nobody reopened. A stand-down fires only on evidence: the measuring step must have SUCCEEDED and reported an explicit `false`, never merely "not `true`", which is satisfied by the empty output a canceled or timed-out job leaves behind. Where a filing step weighs several arms, the stand-down weighs the same ones.
- **Only a check scored against the commit in front of it CLOSES its thread** — `preview-e2e` and `modulepreload-coverage`. The tiers that score against committed baselines (`test/oracle/*.json`, the golden PDFs, the overflow clip ratchet, the Playwright screenshot snapshots) comment and leave the thread open, because blessing a baseline turns them green without fixing anything and the run cannot tell a fix from a re-bless. `perf-nightly` comments for the differential reason: it compares head against a base ~24h old, so night 2 is clean because the base carries the regression too.
- The two `studio-e2e-nightly.yml` jobs additionally require a non-zero passed count before acting. With `OPEN_ROUTER_KEY` unset the live-AI spec skips every test and exits 0, so step outcome and output both read green while nothing at all was tested.
- Every rolling-issue lookup across the six workflows moved off GitHub's `--search "in:title [marker]"`, which tokenizes on brackets and hyphens and matches human-authored issues, onto a client-side title match. A filing step and a stand-down step in the same job now provably resolve to the same issue, and a job's own lookup provably matches the title it creates.
