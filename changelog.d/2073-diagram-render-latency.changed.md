- Editing a Mermaid fence redraws the diagram about two and a half times faster: a keystroke inside a fence now reaches the redrawn diagram in ~80ms where it took ~194ms, a burst in ~78ms where it took ~1.1s, and arriving at a diagram slide takes ~223ms where it took 240ms. The fixed 150ms debounce in front of every diagram render is gone for diagrams that are cheap to draw — under ~50ms of render the diagram now updates live on every keystroke. A costlier diagram still waits that same 150ms, so it cannot be slower than before, and it gains a ceiling it never had: steady typing used to starve the redraw entirely.
- A diagram that does not parse *while you are still typing* no longer blanks the slot and shows you raw source on every keystroke. The previous drawing stays on screen, and the error box appears once you stop (about 450ms of quiet). A `mermaid.parse` check in front of the render is what makes this cheap — it rejects a half-written diagram in ~1.8ms where drawing one costs 22-239ms. It is not free: Mermaid re-parses inside `render`, so a diagram that parses cleanly pays about 14% more than it did, and a diagram that is broken the first time it is drawn now shows its error after that same ~450ms rather than immediately.
- A diagram that has already failed keeps its error box on screen while you edit a different diagram on the same slide, instead of blinking off every time you type. Exporting a deck now waits for diagrams to stop making progress rather than for a fixed four seconds, so a slow deck can no longer bake a blank space into the PDF where a diagram should be.
