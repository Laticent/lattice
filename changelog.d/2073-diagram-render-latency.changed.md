- Editing a Mermaid fence redraws the diagram about two and a half times faster: a keystroke inside a fence now reaches the redrawn diagram in ~78ms where it took ~194ms, and arriving at a diagram slide takes ~119ms where it took 240ms. The fixed 150ms debounce in front of every diagram render is gone — it was 72% of the wait, and larger than a *full render* for any diagram up to about 64 nodes. In its place the render backs off by what a render actually cost: nothing at all below ~50ms, where the diagram updates live on every keystroke and that is what makes editing a small one feel instant; above it, twice the recent median render, capped at 150ms. A fence with nothing on screen yet — first paint, or arriving at a slide — is never made to wait.
- A diagram that does not parse *while you are still typing* no longer blanks the slot and shows you raw source on every keystroke. The previous drawing stays on screen, and the error box appears once you stop (about 450ms of quiet). A `mermaid.parse` check in front of the render is what makes this cheap — it rejects a half-written diagram in ~1.8ms where drawing one costs 22-239ms. It is not free: Mermaid re-parses inside `render`, so a diagram that parses cleanly pays about 14% more than it did, and a diagram that is broken the first time it is drawn now shows its error after that same ~450ms rather than immediately.
- **Known cost:** on a large diagram, the wait after you stop typing is unchanged at a normal typing pace and about 90-130ms longer at a brisk one. That is the parse check paid on top of the same 150ms wait, and it is what buys not flashing raw source at you mid-word.
