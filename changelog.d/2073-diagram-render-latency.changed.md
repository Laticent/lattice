- Editing a Mermaid fence updates the diagram about three times faster: a keystroke inside a fence now reaches the redrawn diagram in ~60ms where it took 209ms, and arriving at a diagram slide takes ~99ms where it took 240ms. The fixed 150ms debounce in front of every diagram render is gone — it was 72% of the wait, and larger than a full render for any diagram up to about 64 nodes. Renders now coalesce on COMPLETION instead: at most one in flight, and if the source moved while it ran, it runs again with the latest text. A diagram render also now backs off by what a render of THAT diagram last cost and by how fast you are typing, so a large diagram is not redrawn on every keystroke while you write. Below ~50ms of render there is no back-off at all and the diagram updates live as you type; above it the wait is the longer of twice the last render and your own measured typing gap, and a fence with nothing on screen yet — arriving at a slide, first paint — is never made to wait. Without that, removing the fixed debounce moved the stall from the diagram into the editor: on a 64-node diagram an eight-character burst bought eight renders and took 2.7s instead of 1.0s, because each keystroke was waiting on the render the previous one started. Editing a small diagram is much faster than before (a keystroke reaches the redrawn diagram in ~78ms where it took ~194ms, and a burst in ~77ms where it took ~1.1s). On a LARGE diagram, the wait after you stop typing is unchanged at a 200-350ms pace and about 90-130ms longer at a brisk 120ms one — the price of no longer flashing raw source at you mid-word.
- A diagram that does not parse *while you are still typing* no longer blanks the slot and shows you raw source on every keystroke. The previous drawing stays on screen, and the error box appears once you stop (about 450ms of quiet). A `mermaid.parse` check in front of the render is what makes this cheap — it rejects a half-written diagram in ~1.8ms where drawing one costs 22-239ms. It is not free: Mermaid re-parses inside `render`, so a diagram that parses cleanly pays about 14% more than it did. A diagram that is broken the first time it is drawn now shows its error after that same ~450ms rather than immediately — the trade that keeps the common case from strobing.
