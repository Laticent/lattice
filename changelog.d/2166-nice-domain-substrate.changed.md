- The padded-domain axis algorithm — the one a chart uses when its marks encode POSITION rather than length — now lives once in the chart substrate, as `cartesian.js § niceDomain`. It had grown twice: in full inside `scatter`, and as a thinner `niceTicks({ tight: true })` flag that `slope` used. Both docblocks argued for themselves with the same anecdote, which is what a duplicate looks like from the inside. `scatter` and `bubble` render pixel-identical after the move; the `tight` flag is deleted rather than left unused.
- `slope` and `dumbbell` inherit the four behaviors the `tight` flag lacked: a target sweep instead of one fixed tick target, a hard tick ceiling, a zero wall that keeps a negative gridline off a quantity that cannot be negative, and a snap-out that reaches the next round tick when it is within a quarter-step. Across every slope and dumbbell slide in the shipped decks that changes one tick set — a slopegraph over 28–71% gains a third gridline — and that slide renders pixel-identical, because a slopegraph draws no gridlines: its ticks only fix the value format, and the vertical scale comes from the data. The one dumbbell slide, which does grid its axis, keeps its `40 · 50 · 60`; only its domain widens, from 6% of air at each end to the family's 8%, which gives the values printed at both endpoints room to set.
