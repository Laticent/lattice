- **`heatmap` prints each cell's value again, and its ink is now solved per palette.** The ramp is five stops rather than a continuous gradient, and every theme carries an ink derived against the fill each stop actually paints (`--heatmap-stepN` / `--heatmap-stepN-ink`, generated by `tools/derive-chart-cat-ink.js` and gated by its `--check` inside `build:check`). Measured on the exported artifact across all 33 palettes on both canvases: **15,444 printed values, none below WCAG AA**, worst `4.59:1`. The two palettes that failed before — `concrete` at `4.19:1` and `a11y-achromatopsia` at `4.49:1` — now clear at `4.62:1` and `4.65:1`.
- Quantizing is what makes this possible rather than a side effect of it. A continuous ramp passes through a band where black and white are equally weak and the best ink of *any* color reaches about `4.6:1`; banding the ink over a continuous fill leaves 58–73 bands below the floor at every band count from 3 to 12. Five chosen stops step over that band — 4 of 95 stops move off even spacing to do it, none by more than 2%.
- **Values close together can now share a stop.** A matrix whose numbers cluster reads as fewer distinct tones than it did on the continuous ramp. That is the cost of the guarantee, and it is the classed-choropleth tradeoff: if a reader has to tell two near-equal cells apart exactly, the comparison belongs on an axis.
- Themes generated in the Studio carry the ramp too — the five stops and five inks joined the token contract rather than riding a `var()` fallback, so a saved theme round-trips them and a theme without them can no longer paint body ink on a saturated cell.
