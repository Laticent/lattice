- **`heatmap` prints each cell's value again, and its ink is now solved per palette.** The ramp is five stops rather than a continuous gradient, and every theme carries an ink derived against the fill each stop actually paints (`--heatmap-stepN` / `--heatmap-stepN-ink`, generated by `tools/derive-chart-cat-ink.js` and gated by its `--check` inside `build:check`). Measured on the exported artifact across all 33 palettes on both canvases: **15,444 printed values, none below WCAG AA**, worst `4.59:1`. The two palettes that failed before — `concrete` at `4.19:1` and `a11y-achromatopsia` at `4.49:1` — now clear at `4.62:1` and `4.65:1`.
- Quantizing is what makes this possible rather than a side effect of it. A continuous ramp passes through a band where black and white are equally weak and the best ink of *any* color reaches about `4.6:1`; banding the ink over a continuous fill leaves 58–73 bands below the floor at every band count from 3 to 12. Five chosen stops step over that band — 4 of 95 stops move off even spacing to do it, none by more than 2%.
- **Values close together can now share a stop, and the stops are cut at the matrix's own quantiles.** A matrix whose numbers cluster reads as fewer distinct tones than it did on the continuous ramp — that is the cost of the guarantee, and it is the classed-choropleth tradeoff: if a reader has to tell two near-equal cells apart exactly, the comparison belongs on an axis. Cutting at quantiles rather than at even slices of the range is what keeps that cost small on the skewed data a heatmap usually carries. Measured across every heatmap slide we ship, equal-interval classing used all five tones on **1 of 8 slides** (mean 4.13 of 5); quantile uses all five on **8 of 8**. A retention table is the worst case and shows why: its M0 column is 100% by construction, so it pins the top of the range while every value the headline is about clusters low. The classing changes no color — the ink is solved per stop and the stops are fixed mix percentages — so the contrast measurement above is unaffected, re-measured at the same worst `4.59:1` with none below AA.
- Themes generated in the Studio carry the ramp too — the five stops and five inks joined the token contract rather than riding a `var()` fallback, so a saved theme round-trips them and a theme without them can no longer paint body ink on a saturated cell.
- **The print band gets its own ramp.** `section.print` is a third canvas that `light-dark()` cannot reach — it remaps `--bg`, `--muted-mark` and `--chart-cat1` to the universal grayscale band, so every input to the cell's fill moves while the ink stays pinned to its light arm. The ink solved against the theme's fill therefore painted on a fill it had never seen: measured, 47 of 95 stop pairs below AA in print, worst `1.40:1` on `carbone`. The band now carries its own five stops and five inks, generated into `base.tokens.css` and gated the same way — measured on the print export, 450 values across five palettes, none below AA, worst `4.65:1`.
- **A value that would not fit is dropped, never truncated.** The fit test used to be geometric (`cellW >= 18 && cellH >= 12`) and could not fail — the shared box always yields 20.9 units of cell — so long values silently ellipsized to `123…`, which still reads as a number and misleads. The test is now per cell, on the formatted string, and against the advance of the face the slide will actually paint — `mode: sketch` sets about 50% wider than the clean face, and measuring with the clean constant let five-digit values overlap. **A dropped value is dropped, not relocated:** the `<desc>` carries each row's peak and the matrix's extremes, not every crossing, so on a grid too dense to print numbers most of them are not recoverable from the slide. Dropping is still better than printing `123…`, which reads as a number and is wrong — but if the numbers matter individually, the grid is too dense for this component.
