- **The `spectrum:` and `accent:` registers no longer repaint a frame that paints its own canvas.** `section.dark.spectrum-off` `(0,2,1)`, `section.dark:is(.spectrum-edge-*)` `(0,3,1)` and `section.accent.dark` `(0,2,1)` all out-specified a frame's own `(0,1,1)` canvas, so unlike the `section.dark` half fixed in #2293 no exemption list could have saved the frame — these won on **specificity**, not on source order. Measured on a real render, indaco, `examples/dark-canvas-ownership.md` with `spectrum: off`: `title`, `topic` and `closing` were `rgb(0,29,51)` and are `rgb(0,61,102)`, the panel they paint for themselves. Identical under `spectrum-edge: left`.
- **None of the three was trying to paint a canvas, which is what made the fix small.** Each draws or removes a *bar* — the dark canvas's top hairline, the divider's left rail, the accent stripe — and restated the color while doing it: two through the `background:` shorthand (chosen because it resets every longhand at once) and one by copying `background-color: var(--bg)` out of the rule it replaced. They declare `background-image` only now, so the bar goes and the canvas stays. #2291 framed the choice as "drop the specificity, or grow `divider`'s carve-out to all four anchors"; both treat the color reset as a given, and it never was.
- **`divider` stops being a special case.** Its two hand-written carve-outs (`section.divider:not(.light).spectrum-off` and `.spectrum-edge-off`) are now the same statement as the others — clear the rail, keep the canvas — rather than the one frame somebody had noticed.
- **An accent cover under `accent` gets its ground back, which was the accessibility half.** A cover keeps `color: var(--on-accent)`; losing the accent ground under `section.accent.dark` left dark ink on a dark ground. Measured, indaco: `decision-cover dark accent` went from `rgb(0,29,51)` to `rgb(130,200,229)` — the same 1.52:1 → 6.10:1 defect #2293 fixed one rule over.
- **`--fin-canvas` loses every register exclusion it carried.** Nine arms in `base.finish.css` each carried `:not(:where(.dark:is(.accent, .spectrum-off, .spectrum-edge-left, .spectrum-edge-right, .spectrum-edge-bottom, .spectrum-edge-off)))`, mirroring rules that no longer repaint anything. What is left is `:not(.print)`, and only on the frames that genuinely lose the surface to print. The trackless `topic` arm carries no condition at all. `finish-canvas-contract.test.js`'s `REGISTERS` list is now empty, and that emptiness is the assertion.
- **`divider` under print is consistent with `title` and `closing` now, and that is a visible change.** Its print exclusion used to be conditional (`.print:not(.spectrum-off):not(.spectrum-edge-off)`) because the spectrum carve-outs restated `--surface-inverse` and so survived print. They restate nothing now, so a printed divider takes the print ground whatever the spectrum register says — which is what a printed title and closing already did.
- **These rules clear the whole bar — four longhands, not one.** A first cut declared only `background-image: none` and argued the geometry longhands were inert with no image to apply to. They are inert only while nothing else supplies an image, and something else can: a deck carries `<style>`, a theme is a stylesheet, and `dist/marp-kit/lattice.css` ships these rules to renderers we do not control. Measured with an author rule setting a full-bleed `background-image` on the section, `divider dark spectrum-off` rendered it as a **3.75px vertical rail** and `content dark spectrum-off` as a **1px strip**. `background-position`, `-size` and `-repeat` are reset with the image now; `background-color` is the one longhand left alone, which is the whole point. Found by the HARD RULE #25 checker.
- Gated by the same 60,263-cell computed-value cross as #2294, plus `spectrum-shorthand-safety.test.js`, which now pins `section.accent.dark` as a rule that declares a stripe and **no** canvas — the stronger form of #1528's guarantee, since a rule with no canvas cannot lose one. Two mutants die: re-adding the canvas line, and restoring either `background:` shorthand.
