- **The Guide's focus now takes color.** The part the narration names takes the accent (somber's takes the heading ink) while the rest steps back to 0.7, so the emphasis is visible at phone size. Measured on all 33 themes in both modes: the accent as text, the highlight's text on its band and the heading ink all hold 4.5:1, and receded text holds 3:1; `test/unit/palette/guide-contrast.test.js` keeps it that way. Chart shapes still recede to the chart hover's 0.45.
- The Guide can also highlight a part (a soft accent band behind it) or ring it (an outline, nothing else changes). Neither changes any box's size.
- Vetrina has two new gestures: `encircle`, a hand-drawn loop fitted to its target (a circle for a compact one, an ellipse for a wide one), and `connect`, a curved arrow from one part to another with the hand traveling it.
- Every component manifest now declares a `gesture`: which of ten structures it renders (statement, list, grid, table, compare, three chart shapes, flow, media) and any unit its render names differently. A gate checks that each component's gallery draws its archetype's primary unit.
- The Studio loads about 11 KB less JavaScript on first load: it shipped the Vetrina walkthrough engine twice (its tours read the prebuilt copy, the demo read the source), and now every docs import reads the source.
- **The Guide now focuses prose, bullets and table rows from what the narration was built from**, not by matching the spoken words against the slide. Each sentence carries the heading, paragraph, item or row it was read from, in Present and in a webpage exported from the Studio, and a slide with no component class plays as a statement. What steps back around the focused part is unchanged: a bullet's siblings, a row's other cells, never the headline. A sentence the builder could not trace to one element, or a caption that replaces the narration, still falls back to matching words. The CLI export does not carry these bindings yet.
- **In Present, the Guide's focus now lands with the word instead of just after it.** It moves to each sentence ahead of the reader, by a lead that calibrates itself to the device: Present measures how long each focus takes to reach the screen, and the lead is the recent median of that delay plus the fade's settle (starting at 120 ms, capped at 300 ms). Measured on desktop Chromium it settles near 90 ms. The focused part fades in fast and settles slowly, so it is fully on as the first word is heard. The captions and the word-by-word read-along keep the audio's own timing.
