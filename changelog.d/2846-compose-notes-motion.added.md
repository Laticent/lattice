- Compose edits a note in place. Opening a `say:`, `describe:` or plain note puts its words in a field with the caret already in it (on a touch screen the note opens for reading, and a tap on the words starts writing, so the keyboard does not rise on every tap); Ctrl/Cmd+Enter or leaving the field writes them, and Escape restores them. Remove stays beside it. Only the words change: the comment's opener, closer and `say:` or `describe:` prefix keep their bytes, and a note that is opened and left is never written. A plain note that would end the comment early, read as a directive (`footer:`, `class:`, `_class:`), a panes marker or `stress-slide`, or a `say:`/`describe:` note that would switch channel or be empty, is refused with the reason and the words stay in the field. The three channels now draw different icons (speech bubble, eye, folded note).
- The Insert menu gains **Motion**. It opens the Library on its Motion scenes; a pick there adds the scene as a slide after the current one, the way it always has from the Library.
- In Compose, Backspace at the start of a block just after a note (or Delete at the end of the block just before one) now selects the note first; a second press removes it. A caret that only sits beside a note, as after picking a slide in the rail, no longer costs the author the note. Typing, pasting or composing text while a note is selected goes to the nearest text instead of replacing the note, and a note on a folded slide opens its panel in view.
- Folding a slide while a note is half typed writes the words first (they were lost), and Shift or Ctrl with Backspace or Delete beside a note also selects it first. Picking a slide that holds only a note leaves the caret on that slide.
- The note field keeps a note's line breaks: a note that spans lines is shown without its hanging indent and written back with it, so changing one word changes that word only. Any slide control (delete, fold, add, settings) writes half-typed words before it acts; Undo and Redo step the history as it stands. Tapping another note writes the open one first. Choosing Motion again brings the Library back to its Motion tab after the author moved to another one.
