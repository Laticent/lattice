- Compose keeps the line breaks inside a paragraph you edit. The engine draws each soft line break as a `<br>`, and Compose used to write a space, so editing a paragraph on slides like `math.gallery.md` ran its lines together. A soft break is now its own node and writes a newline; a hard break (backslash, newline) writes what it always did. A corpus test over the shipped decks holds the engine's break count equal before and after the round trip.
- Editing a paragraph in Compose no longer turns text at the start of a later line into structure: `-`, `+`, `>`, `#`, `1.`, `1)`, a marker after spaces, and a bare `=` or `-` line stay literal. A two-line setext heading keeps its line break and level, and a heading that ends in `#` keeps its escape.
- A hard break next to a soft break (the text between them deleted) writes one hard break; it used to write a blank line that split the paragraph and left a visible backslash.
