- Safari 17.5-17.6 (WebKit 17.4) now draws hub-spoke icons and paints the plugin painting canvas and the plugin-off chip. Each rule's `@supports` guard tested relative color on a plain `red`. WebKit 17.4 passes that test, but it drops a relative color over a `light-dark()` origin, and it rejects `calc()` in a hue channel. So the icon stroke computed to `none` and both backgrounds went blank. Each guard now tests the rule's own shape, so WebKit 17.4 takes the plain inks, while Chromium 153, Firefox 142 and WebKit 26 compute the same values as before. The topic shelf was measured and already paints correctly in WebKit 17.4; its guard now tests the rule's own `max(l, 0.34)`.
- Hub-spoke icons in Safari 17.5-17.6 now take the ink each finish curates for their disc. The finish repaints a disc on every engine, but the icon's ink waited for relative color, so in WebKit 17.4 an icon kept the plain slide's ink on a finished disc: 218 of 528 icons over 32 palettes and three finishes fell under 3:1, the worst 1.11:1. Pigment's group and neutral inks, every finish's status ink and tone's deepest-slot ink need no relative color, so they now apply everywhere; measured again, no icon falls under 3:1 in WebKit 17.4, and Chromium 153 paints every icon exactly as before.
- The same old guards also left hub-spoke icons undrawn in older Chromium and Firefox. Measured over the same 32 palettes and three finishes, Chromium 125 and Firefox 128 drew none of 576 icons before this change; now they draw every icon at 3:1 or better (worst 3.24:1). Both pass a relative-color guard on a plain `red` but drop the color over a `light-dark()` origin, as Safari 17 does.
