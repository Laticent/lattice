- **Dark mode no longer repaints a frame that paints its own canvas.** `section.dark` set the deck-wide canvas at the same `(0,1,1)` specificity as a frame's own rule, and component stylesheets are bundled before `base.modifiers.css` — so at equal specificity source order decided it and the deck ground won. Nine frames are now exempt: the four anchors (`title`, `divider`, `closing`, `topic`) and the five accent covers the splitter emits (`decision-cover`, `compare-code-cover`, `compare-split-cover`, `list-tabular-cover`, `split-panel-cover`). A tenth painter, `lat-split-cover`, needs no entry — it is declared after the rule and already wins.
- **The accent covers were an accessibility defect, not only a cosmetic one.** A cover keeps `color: var(--on-accent)` — the theme's inverse surface under dark — while losing its accent ground, so it rendered dark ink on a dark ground. Measured in Chromium on indaco: `decision-cover` and `split-panel-cover` both went from **1.52:1 to 6.10:1**.
- **`divider` gets its spectrum rail back** — `background-size` was collapsing from `6px 100%` at the default 16:9 export to the deck-wide `100% 1px` hairline, and it had been shipping that way in `divider.gallery.dark.pdf`.
- **A dark split cover now draws the spectrum bar above it.** The old rule carried `border-top: none` deck-wide; the four anchors set their own, the five covers did not. The same covers already draw that bar in light mode, so this brings dark into line rather than adding a treatment.
- A new gate, `test/unit/palette/dark-canvas-ownership.test.js`, parses the built bundle with css-tree — source order is a property of `dist/lattice.css`, so no source-tree walk can ask the question — and fails on a painter declared ahead of the rule without an exemption, on an entry that no longer paints, and on `lat-split-cover` moving ahead of the rule.
- **Not fixed: the `spectrum:` register repaints the same frames** through `section.dark.spectrum-off` and `section.dark:is(.spectrum-edge-*)`, which out-specify a frame's own canvas rather than merely outranking it on order. The *surface* is unchanged here and still flattens; the finish above is excluded across those registers so it follows whatever the slide really paints. Tracked as #2291.
- **A finish on a dark bookend or accent cover now composites against the surface that slide actually paints.** `--fin-canvas` (#1656) names the color every finish layer mixes toward, and it excluded `.dark` because `section.dark` used to repaint a bookend — so once the exemption above landed, a finish on a dark `title` mixed toward the deck ground while the slide painted `--surface-inverse`. The five accent covers were never named in that file at all. Both follow their own canvas now.
- **`topic` is named in that file for the first time.** It paints `--surface-inverse` in every mode and had never been listed, so a finish on a topic slide composited against the deck ground.
- The re-point applies **only where the frame keeps its canvas**, which is a per-frame question: `print` remaps the consumed tokens and `section.print` resets the surface, while `accent` / `spectrum-off` / `spectrum-edge-*` out-specify a frame's own rule — but each of those needs `.dark` to fire, so excluding them unconditionally would break light decks. `divider` keeps its canvas wherever its own `(0,3,1)` spectrum carve-outs beat the repainter, and `topic` takes three arms — `section.topic:not(:has(> ul.tile-track))` restates its canvas at `(0,2,2)`, so a topic carrying its track loses it as `title` does, except as `.fact`.
- Measured over 18 frames × 219 modifier classes × {plain, dark} plus every register pair, in both DOM shapes — 9,918 cells per palette, repeated on all 33 palettes in Chromium 131. Five representative palettes were re-run in Firefox 142 and WebKit 26, where all three engines agree cell for cell, computed `rgb()` values included. Outside the two families tracked as #2294, `main` leaves **26–759** rows per palette compositing against a color the slide does not paint, and this leaves **0** on every one.
- **A `fact` topic keeps its panel under print, and that took a third arm.** `section.topic.fact` `(0,2,1)` restates topic's canvas above `section.print` `(0,1,1)`, so a fact slide holds its inverse panel where a plain tracked topic correctly gives it up — and a blanket `:not(.print)` on the tracked arm sent `--fin-canvas` to white over a surface still painting `rgb(236,236,236)`. That is the same #1656 failure this change exists to remove, on a shape the engine really emits: `fact` hides the track with `display: none`, but the `<ul>` is still in the DOM, so `:has(> ul.tile-track)` matches. Present on `main` in both DOM shapes; the arm that shipped fixed the trackless half and left the half that ships. The exclusions on the new arm were measured across all seven registers rather than copied from the arm beside it.
- **The `topic` arms moved into their own rule block**, because Selectors-4 invalidates a whole selector list on one unparsable complex selector. While topic's `:has()` arms shared a list with `title`, `closing` and `divider`, a renderer that does not understand `:has()` dropped the declaration for those three too — frames that were immune before this file learned about topic. `dist/marp-kit/lattice.css` ships the rule to third-party Marp renderers, so the split bounds the loss to topic for one duplicated declaration.
- **The gate that is supposed to catch the next one can no longer be disarmed in silence.** Its shape derivation was a regex demanding `tag.class`, with no self-check — collapse the topic split AND neuter the derivation and it passed green. It now reads the bundle with css-tree, refuses to guess at a `:has()` form it cannot build into a probe element (and fails naming it), and asserts the tracked-topic shape is still there. A new `variant x register` axis covers the pairing that hid the `fact` defect from both gates, and the anti-vacuity check now names the DOM shape it inspects — it had been silently reading the trackless topic, never the shape that ships.
