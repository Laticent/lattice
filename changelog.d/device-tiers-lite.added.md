- **Studio: Lite mode looks lighter.** On a device the Studio reads as Lite (a software-rendered browser, 4 GB or less, 4 cores or less, or a slow speed test), the slide preview's loading screen is the landing page's single pulsing placeholder instead of the three-layer Nacre animation, no panel or bar uses a backdrop blur (the site header and chart pop-ups turn opaque), and a picture still loading in a live slide shows a still painting rather than moving brushwork. Full mode is unchanged, and exports are byte-for-byte the same in both modes. `?tier=lite` and `?tier=full` pin either mode for a page load.
- **Studio: Lite mode uses less memory.** A Lite device runs exports with one picture encoder and one slide in flight, packs narration with one worker, keeps 6 to 12 live slide previews instead of 10 to 28, prefetches no panels or fonts on a guess, and does not offer the downloaded on-device models (Kokoro voice, WebLLM and Transformers.js chat; they show why they are off). Exports are the same files in both modes, only slower on Lite.
- **Studio: the device check ranks the graphics chip and watches start-up.** The Studio now ranks the graphics chip with detect-gpu, using tables served by Lattice itself (never a CDN), and notices when it fell behind while starting up. Either can switch a session to Lite mode, with a short notice saying why. The System panel shows the GPU rank (or why there is none, such as a chip newer than detect-gpu's tables, Save-Data or a Mac's masked GPU name) and the start-up result.
- **Studio and Playground: a browser Lattice can't run gets told so.** Instead of a broken page, it gets a full-page notice naming what is missing (for example CSS `:has()`) and what to use instead, with "Copy system report" and a small "Continue anyway" that runs the rest of the session in Lite mode under a one-line banner. Workspace settings has a new Performance mode setting: Auto, Full or Lite.
