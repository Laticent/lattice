- On an iPhone, iPad or Mac Safari, a Studio PowerPoint or LibreOffice export of a deck with phone photos came out with the wrong layout, a larger file, and sometimes the wrong pictures. Three WebKit-only causes, all fixed. (1) The `image` layout never read the size of a photo over about half a megabyte, because WebKit's regex engine returns no match on the pattern that found its address; the slide kept its fallback layout with a bigger photo box. Both address readers (`lib/core/image-aspect.js`, `lib/core/css-url.js`) are now plain scans that return what the old patterns did, at any length. (2) The export could take a slide's canvas before WebKit had drawn it, so a LibreOffice file repeated slide 2's picture on every slide after it and a PowerPoint title slide came out blank; the slide camera now makes WebKit finish the draw before the canvas leaves it. (3) The browser shrank a 12-megapixel photo itself inside the slide, and WebKit left grain a JPEG cannot compress.
- The slide camera now shrinks each large photo to the pixels it covers on the slide before drawing it, in halving steps, as lossless PNG, so no engine scales it and the result is the same on every browser. Measured on a two-photo deck of 12-megapixel photos (PowerPoint): Studio on WebKit 1,286 KB → see the PR; Studio on Chromium 817 KB → 648 KB; command line 1.6 MB → 1.2 MB. Against an ideal high-quality resize of the original photo, the slide's photo is closer than before on every engine (Chromium 24.6 → 26.6 dB). A photo shown at or near its own size, a vector picture, and the PDF's embedded originals are unchanged.
- The Studio export holds the shot until an `image` slide's photo size is known (capped at 4 s, as before), through the measure registry it already waits on.
