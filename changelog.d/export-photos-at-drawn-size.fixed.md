- On an iPhone, iPad or Mac Safari, a Studio PowerPoint or LibreOffice export of a deck with phone photos came out with the wrong layout, a larger file, and sometimes the wrong pictures. Three WebKit-only causes, all fixed. (1) The `image` layout never read the size of a photo over about half a megabyte, because WebKit's regex engine returns no match on the pattern that found its address; the slide kept its fallback layout with a bigger photo box. Both address readers (`lib/core/image-aspect.js`, `lib/core/css-url.js`) are now plain scans that return what the old patterns did, at any length. (2) The export could take a slide's canvas before WebKit had drawn it, so a LibreOffice file repeated slide 2's picture on every slide after it and a PowerPoint title slide came out blank; the slide camera now makes WebKit finish the draw before the canvas leaves it, and the Studio sends its encode workers the canvas's pixels instead of an `ImageBitmap`, which WebKit delivered blank. (3) The browser shrank a 12-megapixel photo itself inside the slide, and WebKit left grain a JPEG cannot compress.
- The slide camera now shrinks a photo that has twice or more the pixels it covers on the slide, itself, with a Lanczos-3 resize, as lossless PNG, before the slide is drawn; a photo under twice is left to the browser as before. Measured against the original resized with Lanczos, in the real camera: a 12-megapixel photo at 3x its box went from 27.6 dB and SSIM 0.827 to 49.2 dB and 0.9985 on WebKit, and from 37.1 dB and 0.974 to 40.7 dB and 0.989 on Chromium. In the Studio's PowerPoint of a three-photo deck of 12-megapixel photos, Chromium went from 583 KB to 491 KB, and WebKit now writes 492 KB (on `main` it laid the slides out wrong). Vector pictures and the PDF's embedded originals are unchanged.
- The Studio export holds the shot until an `image` slide's photo size is known (capped at 4 s, as before), through the measure registry it already waits on.
