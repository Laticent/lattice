- **Breaking: a square, portrait, story or mobile deck with no `venue:` now renders at the new `phone` venue.** `phone` and `tablet` join `laptop`, `huddle`, `conference` and `hall`, and the deck's shape sets each venue's type step (`VENUE_SCALE_BY_FAMILY` in `lib/core/resolve-venue.js`). On a square deck the phone default is 1.5x, about 16pt on a phone, where `laptop` read at about 11pt; write `venue: laptop` to keep the old size. A portrait, story or mobile deck's default size does not change. A 16:9 deck keeps `laptop` as its default and its room steps. A chart slide keeps `laptop` on every shape for now: at phone, 40 chart slides that fit at laptop clipped, and charts move to phone with their narrow-box reflow.
- **Tall decks rise less at a room venue:** portrait, story and mobile are 1.1x at huddle, 1.17x at conference and 1.22x at hall (were 1.15x, 1.3x and 1.5x), so a story at hall keeps about 35 characters a line. Across the galleries, clipped slides at hall fall from 364 to 196 on portrait and from 218 to 81 on story.
- **Venue is the only thing that changes a text size.** The split cover's lead-in and a `topic fact` claim no longer step down a size in a narrow box, and a lone item on a split page no longer steps up to the emphasis size. The one exception is math's lead equation, which scales to fit because a single expression cannot wrap.
- **Long words break with a dash on square, portrait, story and mobile slides,** instead of running past the edge: words of 10 or more letters hyphenate at a syllable with a dash, and a word the dictionary cannot split is never broken without one. Code and math keep their characters whole. 16:9 slides are unchanged. The PDF export draws the dash and places the second half on its own line, as the browser does.
- **Fixed:** a big-number caption on a tall slide wraps across the full width instead of a dozen characters a line; a glossary term wraps between its words in a narrow box, so a long term no longer squeezes its definition.
- **Lint and the Studio know the new venues:** `unknown-venue` lists `phone` and `tablet`; capacity budgets read each venue at the measured size its shape renders; the Studio's venue picker, the Present stage's venue switch and the clip notice's "smaller venue" fix follow the deck's shape and show the phone default.
- **A progress chart splits like a list when it overflows a square, portrait or story slide.** A page holds as many bars as fit (up to four, or the measured count under `fit: pack`), no page holds a lone bar, and every page draws its bars against the same track. The run opens on a cover reading "Where each one stands". Before, the bars ran off the bottom of the slide.
- **Timelines run left to right on a 16:9 slide and down the page on every other shape, at every venue.** A `timeline-list` chart and an `inventory timeline` list now switch on the same rule: hd, 4K and standard keep the row; square, portrait, story and mobile stack the milestones down a left rail, whatever the venue. Before, a square timeline-list at laptop kept four columns about ten characters wide while the list timeline beside it was vertical, and a 16:9 list timeline at hall turned vertical. A list timeline in a narrow pane still runs down the page.
- **A timeline-list splits by milestone when it overflows.** It opens on a cover reading "Where the line starts" and holds several milestones a page, in date order, each page with its own rail.
- **`fit: pack` plans a chart's split pages correctly.** It measured a chart's room as if each page dropped its heading, and planned a horizontal row of milestones down to one a page.
- **Footers, labels and support text read larger on a phone and a tablet.** On every square, portrait, story and mobile deck at the phone or tablet venue, small text (footers, labels, chips) now reads at about 13pt on the device instead of about 11pt, and support lines at about 15pt, beside 17pt body text. Body text and headings keep their size.
- **Slides split by measurement by default.** A deck with no `fit:` line now splits the way `fit: pack` did: on a square, portrait, story or mobile slide, one that fits stays one page, and one that overflows splits into as many items a page as fit, by measured height. Before, it split by a set number of items a page, which split slides that fit and clipped long ones. Page counts change for many decks, usually down. `fit: heal` asks for the count-based split; `trim` and `report` keep their meaning, and a deck with only the old `guards:` key splits by count as before. 16:9 slides never split.
- **Display math has one size and wraps at the slide's real width.** On a square, portrait, story or mobile slide every display equation is set at 1.4 times body text and wraps where the slide runs out, only at safe points (after `=`, `+` or `−` at the top level, or between two matrices; never inside brackets, a fraction or a subscript). Before, a long equation was rewritten onto aligned lines by a length guess and set smaller (1.55em, 1.4em or 1.2em), so math read at three sizes in one deck and the longest still ran off the edge. An equation whose author wrote their own line breaks is left alone, and 16:9 slides are unchanged.
- **Three math layouts fit a square slide.** On a square slide `math stats` runs its reading line the slide's full width, so the estimate no longer slides off the top; `math compare` stacks its formulations in one column, so a split page reads one formulation top to bottom instead of flowing it across two columns; and the function plot on `math canvas` keeps readable axis numbers.
- **A function plot's axis numbers stay readable when the slide's labels are large for the plot.** On a phone or tablet slide the plot is drawn smaller and scaled up to its box, so its margin and tick spacing grow with the labels, and it shows fewer ticks (−6, −4, … instead of every integer). Before, the y labels were cut to `).8` and the x labels ran together. 16:9 plots are unchanged.
- **A square slide at the phone venue stacks its title over the deck's meta line,** as portrait and story slides do. Before, a long `meta:` took the band's width at phone size and squeezed the title to one word a line, running it off the slide. A meta line that wraps now breaks into even lines instead of leaving one word on its own line. A square deck at `venue: laptop` keeps the title and meta side by side.
