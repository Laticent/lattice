- **New `histogram` chart for distributions.** Write each band as a range and its share: `` `$1M..$10M` `55%` ``. Bars touch because the bands join end to end, and each bar's area is its share, so bands of different widths draw honestly. Add `log` to the axis line (`` `[{Annual loss, $1M..$250M, log}]` ``) when the bands widen toward the tail. `lint:deck` names a band written with a dash, bands that leave a gap or overlap, and `log` over a band that starts at zero. Demo deck: `examples/histogram.md`.
- **New `ep-curve` chart for return-period losses.** Write each point as `` `{return period, loss}` ``, such as `` `{100, $410M}` ``, and nest a curve's points under its name for OEP and AEP or gross and net (up to four curves). The return period runs across on a log scale labeled `1-in-10`, `1-in-100`, `1-in-1,000`, and narration reads each curve point by point. Demo deck: `examples/ep-curve.md`.
- **The chart family guide now lists how every chart is written and where its text goes** (`chart-family.docs.md` § How a chart is written): ranges are `a..b`, a position is a `{x, y}` record, an axis is one bracketed line above the heading, and a log axis is the word `log` in it. Its § Axes and labels sets where ticks, axis titles, value labels and series names go, how crowded labels thin or report, and the sizes and baselines they use, each rule naming the code that enforces it.
- **Both charts take a `chart-finish:`.** Under `pigment`, `etch` and `tone`, histogram bars repaint like bar-chart bars do. EP-curve dots repaint under `pigment` and `etch`; under `tone` they keep their curve's color, as line-chart dots do, so a dot never wears a different color from its own curve.
- **Histogram bars look like bar-chart bars.** They use the same soft wash and outline, and a thin background-colored cut separates two touching bars, so neighbors never merge into one shape under any finish, in light or dark.
- **Hovering a histogram bar shows its full outline.** The cuts between bars step aside while a bar is held, and they never catch the pointer, so moving across a bar's edge doesn't close its card. Bars that overlap by mistake also get a cut, so they read as two shapes.
