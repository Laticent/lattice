- Inline pill shapes now match each other. `:circle` and `:diamond` are as tall as the capsule, so a line or a `list-tabular` row that holds one stops growing. Before, they measured 31.9px and 37.9px beside a 25.9px capsule. `:diamond` is now a 1.4:1 rhombus, so it can hold two characters at that height. The chevrons and the diamond draw the same 1px edge as every other shape; before, they rendered as flat tint with no edge. `:tag-bordered` now draws its double stroke inside its own box, where the old outer ring spilled 3px onto the words beside it.
- Pill labels now sit on the visual center of the pill in every browser, not just on the center of the line box. Where the browser supports `text-box` (current Chrome, Firefox and Safari), the label is trimmed to its capitals and centered on them. Measured, the worst pill is now 0.58px off in Chrome, 0.21px in Firefox and 0.42px in WebKit, where Chrome used to put labels up to 1.3px high. Exported PDFs render with Chrome 131, which predates `text-box`, and take a measured fallback: the worst case in the PDF drops from 1.47px to 0.68px. The label is also no longer pushed left by the letter-spacing after its last letter.
- The left chevron's flat right edge no longer disappears in WebKit (Safari and the macOS app) when the pill's width lands on a fractional pixel.
- Inline pills now size themselves from the text they sit in. In a heading a pill is half the heading's size, so it no longer looks tiny beside a title. In a below-note, a caption or a footer it takes the small size. Pills in body text, lists, cards and table cells are unchanged. `:sm` and `:lg` still work and now scale that automatic size.
