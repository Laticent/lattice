- On phones and tablets, the Studio's previews (the main preview, Present, the slide overview, and the Add slide, Reshape and Print grids) now show each photo at the size it is drawn, not at its original size; on a desktop, the small previews do. A 12-megapixel photo is about 48 MB once decoded, whether it fills the preview or a small thumbnail, and an iPhone could discard the tab on decks with many photos. In a WebKit test (the engine of Safari on iPhone and iPad), ten such photos in ten preview frames went from 1,016 MB to 204 MB; Firefox saves about as much, while Chrome, which keeps the memory it used making the copies, saves little. This covers photo backgrounds (`![bg]`) and photos in the text (`![alt](…)`, what the Compose photo picker and the chat agent add), but not yet the photos of a collage, whether they come from the Library (the camera roll), the site's own samples or a web address you have allowed. A web photo you have not allowed is never fetched to make a copy. A background photo's first appearance takes slightly longer while its smaller copy is made: an image panel shows its loading painting meanwhile, and a full-slide background stays blank until the copy arrives. A photo in the text shows its original until its copy is ready, then switches in the same place; the copies of photos on nearby slides are made ahead. Zooming into the preview loads a sharper copy once the zoom settles, up to 1920 pixels wide. A desktop's main preview keeps showing the original photo. A photo from a site that does not allow it to be read keeps loading at full size, as before.
