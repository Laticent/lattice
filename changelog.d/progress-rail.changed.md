- `progress-rail:` front-matter key: place the section rail beside the page number (`right`, the default), in the middle of the footer (`center`) or upright on the right edge (`edge`), and draw it as `dots` or a segmented `bar`. One line, one word per axis, e.g. `progress-rail: edge bar`. `off` hides it and `on` draws it in a shorter deck. The Studio completes each word, and the Deck Inspector sets it under Chrome → Section rail. A slide moves its own rail with `_class: progress-rail-edge`.
- The section rail now draws only where it says something: in a deck of three or more sections and fifteen or more slides, on content slides inside a section. A one- or two-section deck, or a short one, no longer shows it unless it says `progress-rail: on`. Past eight sections it draws as a bar.
- Each divider now draws its own section marker (`2 / 4` and the rail's marks, larger) directly above the section name, taking none of the slide's room, so a reader learns what the footer marks mean where the section is named. An agenda with one item per section shows each item's marks as a legend, and a topic slide with no eyebrow names its section (`02 · Unit economics`). An agenda placed inside a section keeps its footer rail.
- The rail's current mark is in the heading's ink instead of the accent, the same cue the topic track uses for its current topic. A hairline now separates the rail from the page number, so the number no longer reads as one more mark.
- A `divider numbered` numeral now counts every divider, so a deck that mixes plain and numbered dividers prints the same section number the rail lights (it printed `01` on a third section).
- `npm run lint:deck` suggests `empty-section` when a divider follows a divider directly, leaving a section with no slides.
