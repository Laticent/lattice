- The risk matrix's critical band is the darkest band again on the dark face of the achromatopsia, deuteranopia and tritanopia palettes. Since the per-theme status colors it had landed between low and medium there, so a palette that shows severity only by lightness read critical as the second-mildest band.
- On the a11y palettes, the risk matrix's critical key number now clears 4.5:1 in browsers without relative color (Safari 17.5 to 17.x, Firefox 120 to 127), where it had fallen to 2.97-4.42:1 on the dark face.
- In Safari 17.5 and 17.6, text whose ink a chart picks from the color under it (the risk matrix's key numbers, finished pills and labels, a tower's share values) no longer turns black. That WebKit drops a relative color whose origin is a light-dark() color, so such text painted black, 1.0:1 on the light critical band. The engine's guard now tests that shape, and Safari 17.x uses each rule's plain curated ink.
- In the HTML player, an a11y palette's dark slide now shows the palette's own near-black ink on its status marks, as the PDF does. Since the per-theme status colors, the player had used the white ink of onyx, which the a11y palettes extend, so the risk matrix's key numbers read at about 1.1:1 on their discs.
- The same player fix applies to every token an a11y palette fixes over onyx's pairs. On a dark slide in the player, the categorical ramp (`--cat-N-fill`, `--cat-N-mark`), `--cat-on-mark` and `--diagram-critical` now use the a11y palette's own values, as the PDF already did, rather than onyx's dark arms.
- An exported HTML player no longer crashes Chrome 119-122 on a slide with a picked ink, such as a dark risk matrix. Those versions have relative color but no light-dark(), and they crash on a relative color over a color-mix(). The player now keeps the @supports light-dark() probe, so they take the plain, curated ink.
