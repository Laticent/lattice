- **`split-panel` and `split-compare` keep the running header and footer on their own field.** The chrome used to span the whole slide, so a long header crossed the panel seam in the panel's ink and vanished, and on `split-compare` it printed over the option cards. Each now ends at the edge of the field it starts on and holds one line, ending in an ellipsis past that, and the field keeps that band clear so a heading can't grow into it. A cut is reported to the author (the export's `CONTENT CLIPPED` line), with no reader pill, the same policy as the standard footer band.
- **A `finish:` now shows on split slides.** Both panels used to paint opaque fills over the deck's backdrop, so a finish deck's split slides showed no texture, wash or mark. On a finish deck the feature panel keeps its field — dark stays dark, an accent or category panel keeps its color — and the finish is painted on it, composed against that field in the screen and the PDF face alike (the way a title slide carries it). The supporting side stays the plain canvas; on `metric`, whose dark field is the right-hand panel, the dark side carries it and the light number panel stays plain. Portrait and square decks, where the panels stack, keep the solid panel as before, as do decks without a `finish:` and `finish-none` slides.
- **`split-compare` no longer drops the footer, and `no-footer` / `silent` now hide a `split-panel` footer.** The transform used to discard `split-compare`'s footer and nest `split-panel`'s inside the right column, out of reach of the chrome modifiers. An author's own `<footer>` (a quote attribution, a source line) stays where it was written.
- **A `split-panel` quote no longer loses its text to the left panel.** With no lede above it, the export moved the quotation's paragraph into the panel as the lede and left an empty quote behind.
- **A `steps` slide's phase numeral no longer sits under the running header**, and `split-panel metric mirror` inks its header for the dark field it sits on (it read 2.2:1).
- **Inline code is readable on every `split-panel` field.** The chip took the dark panel's white ink on every variant but `metric`, so on a `steps` panel and on each `cat-N` tint — including a category run's split cover — it rendered white on a light fill (the formulas on `system-design-foundations` pp. 125 and 183). `steps` now keeps the ordinary chip ink, a `cat-N` tint takes `--cat-on-fill`, and `watermark` takes `--on-accent`.
