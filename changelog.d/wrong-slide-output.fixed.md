- A split-panel point whose title carries its own bold run (`- **6** again`) no longer leaves a `<strong>` open on the split page. The split now reads the title as a balanced element, so later slides stop rendering bold, the export finds every slide again, and the title keeps its trailing words ("6 again", not "6").
- A `<style>`, `<pre>`, `<textarea>` or `<script>` block closed with a space before the bracket (`</style >`) now ends at that tag. It used to run on, swallow the next `---`, and merge two slides into one, so a lens export could show a withheld slide's body under the next slide's lens tag.
- A front-matter key nested under a block, such as a lens named `footer` under `lenses:`, is no longer read as the deck's directive. That lens registry used to print in every slide's footer and could overwrite the real `footer:`.
- When a deck's front matter repeats a top-level key, Lattice now reads the **last** one everywhere, as the engine always rendered it. That covers the shared front-matter readers (every register such as `finish:`, `motion:` and `class:`), the linter, the Studio, the CLI export's `size:`, `style:` and `logo:`, the Export-to-Marp `class:` and `paginate:`, the color-mode band, block keys such as `lexicon:` and `say:`, the Studio's own front-matter reader and writer (so its controls show and edit the line that counts), and the Studio's lens list; they used to read the first. The render-target keys (`fluid:`, `player:`, `present:`, `read:`) keep their deliberate rule, on if any line says on. A top-level line now also beats a nested one of the same name. `key :` (a space before the colon) counts as the same key, as it does in YAML. `lint:deck` warns on each earlier line (`duplicate-key`), since the deck ignores it. The writers act on every copy too: resetting a setting in the Studio ("Theme default", motion off, a preset) removes every top-level copy of its key, clearing the plugin list removes every `plugins:` line, rather than letting an earlier one take effect again, and the auto-glossary and heading-split exports strip every `glossary:` and `split:` line, so a re-import does not add a second glossary. `--print` export collapses every `color-mode:` line into `color-mode: print`, `color-mode :` included.
