A Lattice **deck** is a Markdown file. Each **slide** picks one **layout** — a
ready-made arrangement — and you fill it with your **content**. One **style**
dresses the whole deck: its colors and its finish. That is the entire model;
everything else is detail you reach for only when you need it.

## The five words you need

You can author a boardroom-grade deck knowing only these.

| Word | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Deck** | Your Markdown file. One deck renders to one PDF (or PPTX, or a set of PNGs). |
| **Slide** | One page. A new heading — or a `---` — starts the next slide. |
| **Layout** | The ready-made arrangement a slide picks: `cards-grid`, `big-number`, `chart`, and so on. You choose one; it does the composition and typography for you. |
| **Content** | Your words. You write plain Markdown; the layout places it. |
| **Style** | The whole deck's look and feel — its palette (the **theme**) and its finish (clean, sketched, and the rest). Set once, up top. |

## Pick a layout, then fill it

You never place boxes by hand. You choose a **layout** for what the slide is
*for* — a number to land, options to weigh, steps to walk — and pour your content
in. The layout is content-aware: it sizes, wraps, and spaces so the slide reads
as *composed*, not typed. When it holds more than it should, it tells you rather
than overflowing.

An author writes one line to pick the layout, then the content beneath it:

```markdown
<!-- _class: big-number -->

## 38%

of pipeline stalls trace to a single approval step.
```

Browse [the layout catalog](/components/) to see every layout, each with its slots
and options, in any palette.

## One style, every slide

Change the **theme** and every slide re-colors. Change the **style** and every
slide takes on the new finish. The layouts never move — color and feel are a coat
of paint on the *outside*, independent of what each slide holds. Swap the coat as
often as you like; your content stays exactly where it was.

That independence is the point of the whole model: what a slide *says* (its
layout and content) and how the deck *feels* (its style) are separate choices, so
changing one never disturbs the other.

:::note[The engine's own words]
Under the hood, the engine calls a layout a **component** and treats every slide
as a few independent choices at once. You never need that to author a deck — it
is the designer's view of the same thing. If you're curious, see
[the concept map](/model/concepts/).
:::
