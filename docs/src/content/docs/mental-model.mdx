A Lattice **deck** is a Markdown file. Each **slide** picks one **layout** — a
ready-made arrangement — and you fill it with your **content**. One **style**
dresses the whole deck: its colors and its finish. That is the entire model;
everything else is detail you reach for only when you need it.

This page is the primer: the model, the handful of words, your first slides, and
how to turn them into a PDF.

<DeckDiagram />

## The five words you need

You can author a boardroom-grade deck knowing only these.

| Word | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Deck** | Your Markdown file. One deck renders to one PDF (or PPTX, or a set of PNGs). |
| **Slide** | One page of the deck. A `---` line separates one slide from the next. |
| **Layout** | The ready-made arrangement a slide picks: `cards-grid`, `big-number`, `chart`, and so on. You choose one; it does the composition and typography for you. |
| **Content** | Your words. You write plain Markdown; the layout places it. |
| **Style** | The whole deck's look and feel — its palette (the **theme**) and its finish (clean, sketched, and the rest). Set once, up top. |

## Your first deck

A deck is just Markdown. A **front-matter** block at the top sets the palette; a
`<!-- _class: … -->` comment picks each slide's **layout**; a `---` line starts
the next slide.

```markdown
---
theme: indaco
paginate: true
---

<!-- _class: title -->

# From Signal to Strategy

A decision framework for product leaders.

---

<!-- _class: big-number -->

## 38%

of pipeline stalls trace to a single approval step.
```

Two slides — a title, then a single number. You wrote words; the layouts placed
them.

## Pick a layout for what the slide is *for*

You never arrange boxes by hand. You choose the layout that matches the slide's
job, and pour your content in:

| The slide's job | Layouts to reach for |
|---|---|
| **Anchor** a section | `title`, `divider`, `closing` |
| **Land one point** | `big-number`, `quote` |
| **Show a set of items** | `cards-grid`, `list`, `agenda` |
| **Weigh options** | `compare-table`, `verdict-grid` |
| **Walk a sequence** | `list-steps`, `roadmap` |
| **Back it with data** | `stats`, `chart`, `diagram` |
| **Show a visual** | `image` |

The layout is content-aware: it sizes, wraps, and spaces so the slide reads as
*composed*, not typed. If you give it more than it's built for, Lattice flags it
(`npm run lint:deck`) rather than letting the slide overflow. Browse
[the layout catalog](/components/) to see every layout, each with its slots and
options, in any palette.

## Style it once

Two front-matter keys dress the whole deck:

- **`theme:`** — the palette. One of 14 (`indaco`, `cuoio`, `onyx`, `laguna`, …),
  each with a light and a dark variant.
- **`mode:`** — the typographic hand: `boardroom` (clean, the default) or
  `sketch` (hand-drawn).

Change either and every slide follows. The layouts never move — color and finish
are a coat of paint on the *outside*, independent of what each slide holds. That
independence is the point of the whole model: what a slide *says* (its layout and
content) and how the deck *feels* (its style) are separate choices, so changing
one never disturbs the other.

## Render it

One command turns your deck into a file. The extension picks the format:

```sh
npx lattice deck.md deck.pdf     # vector PDF
npx lattice deck.md deck.pptx    # PowerPoint (image slides)
npx lattice deck.md deck.png     # one PNG per slide
```
