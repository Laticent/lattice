They are kept in full, losers included, for three reasons. The judge's
recommendation is to graft from the runners-up rather than take the winner
whole, so the losing documents are load-bearing input to whatever ships. Each
carries measurements the others do not — Track 4's redundancy ladder halves the
accessibility work, Track 6 alone scored the gradient decision against
alternatives, Track 2 alone audited the interaction surface, Track 1 alone
rendered and looked. And a proposal that was rejected is the cheapest possible
record of why, for the next session tempted by the same idea.

**Read them as arguments, not as facts.** The fact-checker found real errors in
every one of the six, and they are named per track in `judgement.md` — including
a fabricated measurement in Track 3 and three phantom work items in Track 1.
Several also inherited a wrong number ("eight cartesian members") straight from
the brief, which is why that error is now corrected at source.
