# Judged ranking — the three colour-forward finishes

Brief: [`colour-brief.md`](colour-brief.md). Candidates in `candidates-colour/`.

**Winner: track 3 (systems) — `pigment` / `etching` / `ground`.**

## Track 3 — systems · 8.5/10

Best answer to the brief, and the only track whose architecture actually runs as written. It probed the one CSS fact that decides whether 'a finish is token declarations' is achievable at all — a frame-level role token cannot forward a slot-scoped value — found six of its own declarations dead, restructured (levels as properties, inks as gated rules naming only role classes), and RE-COST UPWARD. I reproduced the probe: the frame-level form computes to black, the gated form resolves. Its separation mechanism is the most universal in the set: every adjacent pair differs on MARK BODY DEPTH (82/30/50), which 20 of 21 members have, plus value ink, plus ground — and the 82->50 drop on `ground` is deliberately designed so `ground` does not ride on its panel alone, which is precisely how finishes.spec.js's `plate` failed. Rule O-prime is the only treatment anywhere of the fact that a per-mark ground COMPOSITES, reintroducing radar's fourth-colour defect on a new register; it answers with one clipped ground per group. It specifies buildGround per substrate rather than deferring it (deferring is the `plate` failure again). Its bearing-cap work is the strongest single piece of evidence in the competition: 52%/62% is AA-clean for --text-heading and --cat-on-fill (0/224) and catastrophic for the four other inks bearing members actually print (--text-secondary 60->224 failures), so it re-points the text rather than lowering the cap — a self-inflicted-regression catch under HARD RULE #18. Two cost findings nobody else has: 19 of 21 members' fills are re-pointable with ZERO transform edits because a CSS rule beats the `fill="url(#…)"` presentation attribute and only pie and quadrant use inline style; and the consequent dead gradient defs riding in every exported SVG/PDF. It alone caught that the chrome ladder is UNBUILT (all four tokens exist only in the failed spec) and refused to claim it as leverage. Weaknesses, all self-declared: `etching`'s central claim — ink at stroke scale reading as strongly as a body — is unfalsified and made a precondition of adoption, correctly; word-cloud is its thinnest member; the light-dark() percentage discovery forces a doubled declaration form that roughly doubles every shared rule consuming a level; 8 members need emitters and 7 need data-ramp. Three fact-check refutations, all peripheral counting (33 not 46 a11y arms, texture width, 'every shared rule is :is(section, figure)' — 15 of 53), none reversing a conclusion. Missing: the magnitude-ramp clause (its body-depth lever is exactly what breaks on map/journey/matrix-grid/progress) and the SVG-only texture fact (its Rule A is unsatisfiable on the 5 HTML members, which its own Rule G newly gives a categorical hue). Both are graftable.

## Track 4 — accessibility-colourist · 7.5/10

The best single CONCEPT in the set and the deepest accessibility work, held back by new machinery and a broken ink layer. 'A finish names the carrier of identity' — FIELD / EDGE+TYPE / FIELD+EMBLEM — makes distinguishability and achromatopsia survival the SAME property: convert any of the three to grayscale and delete every word and you still get solid shapes / outlined shapes / marked shapes. One decision buys both, which is exactly what the brief asks for and what the failed four-finish attempt could not do. Its accessibility findings are original and verified: the a11y patterns paint a LITERAL hex rect before overlaying geometry (so texture and hue are mutually exclusive by construction, and the literal exists because token resolution in page-level defs rendered pies all-black on real iOS Safari); texture is delivered as `fill: url(#pattern)` and is therefore SVG-ONLY, so it can never reach the five HTML members — a fact that invalidates part of every other track's a11y section; and the four CVD palettes override no --chart-cat token at all, so five surfaces get the same eight grays. Its catch that widening only the redundant channel to 8 while pie and funnel cycle COLOUR at 6n manufactures a distinction not in the data — verified — is sharper than Track 3's equivalent assertion, and its gate measures the RENDERED repeat period rather than token counts. The Layer A / Layer B split is a genuine structural contribution: it is the only track to notice that a `pigment` which re-groups four members and colours every name in 21 cannot also be a no-op default, and it resolves that into a shippable sequence rather than conceding it. Its I4 split (area strength varies / bearing wash PINNED / backdrop floored at max(8%, area/3)) is the best resolution of interaction 4 anywhere, catching that one derivation would collapse bullet's band and the bearing wash together. Against it: `--chart-cat-label-ink: var(--chart-slot-ink)` on the frame hits the forwarding trap I confirmed, and --chart-slot-ink is defined nowhere — so half of `ledger`'s named carrier (the TYPE) does not work as written, though its field/edge/emblem levers do. `emblem` is the only proposal adding genuinely new machinery (8 drawn shapes, a placement pass, two new emitted attributes) and it degrades to exactly the failed spec's `plate` wherever the shape cannot be placed — the eleven-slice pie, word-cloud, small map regions — which Track 4 admits. Eleven bearing members separate pigment/ledger only on edge width, accent width and a coloured status word, which it counts honestly rather than conceding one member. One refutation, and it is over-caution: it WITHDREW radar's reading citing candidate 5's claim that the tool is alpha-blind, but the tool now composites stop-opacity (commit 8bf22ff) and radar is measured, and still SWAMPED.

## Track 1 — colourist · 7/10

The strongest evidence base and the most disciplined self-correction, wrapped around an architecture that does not run. Its central finding is real and reproduced across 224 combinations: the ink tier is the most chromatic (1.21x the 82% solid on 28 of 28 theme x mode combinations), the most separable, and the only categorical channel that clears its floor everywhere (4.65:1 worst; the 82% solid falls under the 3:1 graphical floor in 36 of 224). That reframes the whole family correctly — the strongest thing the charts own is their ink. Its epistemic conduct is the best in the set: it withdrew its own adjacent-separation column mid-document when it failed to reproduce, and withdrew the 6%/97% reach figures its earlier draft led with after discovering the tool's own docblock retires chroma as a proxy for reach, replacing them with the keyed metric (216/318, 68%) that shows the real gap is the HTML and status members, not the SVG ones. Its magnitude x strength clause — a ramp member declares lo/hi and a finish SCALES both endpoints — is a catch no other track makes, and it is the exact hole in the winner's mechanism on map, journey, matrix-grid and progress. Its a11y clamp is the cheapest correct answer to the depth problem (measured: at 26% on the grey ramp, 8/8 slots fall under 3:1). But the architecture in section 7 ships `--chart-edge-source: var(--mark-ink)` and `--chart-ink-value: var(--mark-ink)` on the frame — both dead by the trap I confirmed, and those are two of the three levers `ink` and `ground` differ on, so the design does not produce itself. And it fails the brief's central test on its own walk: `pigment` is IDENTICAL to `ink` on word-cloud (the mark axis is inert, there is no rung 3 or 4) and weak on journey, progress and roadmap — 4 of 21 where two finishes converge, which is the failure the brief exists to prevent. Its one refutation is a mode-mismatch artifact (claimed 19-20:1 on the a11y dark canvas; resolving both operands in dark gives 1.01-1.10:1) whose correction strengthens its own clamp argument — measurement hygiene, not fabrication. Section 2's two-name alternative, put on the table with its costs and a recommendation to let the render settle it, is the right posture for a track whose third name buys the least.

## Track 2 — editorial · 5.5/10

The clearest editorial voice and the most immediately legible three-way look, undermined by an accessibility section that is materially unsound. `folio` / `spread` / `survey` as broadsheet / magazine / surveyor's sheet is the most communicable framing in the set, and the M/F/R lever table with a per-member weakest-link column is the most reviewable distinguishability argument. It independently identified the custom-property trap — 'Layers 1 and 2 sit on the SAME selector, and that is load-bearing' — and structured its derivation correctly, which puts it ahead of Tracks 1 and 4 architecturally. It contributes three grafts nobody else has: `chart-finish:` rather than `charts:` (resolve-finish.js already owns a deck-level `finish:` register, so an author reading 'chart finishes' types the colliding word); `vector-effect: non-scaling-stroke` on any doubled edge, since stroke-width is in viewBox units across boxes of 320x180, 420x348 and 300x300; and Rule S1's minimum drawn dimension, since a centred outline consumes the body it bounds on a thin waterfall delta. Its G0 restatement ('does this mark carry its own name?') answers pie, funnel, bar and scatter unaided where the earlier phrasing did not. But: its achromatopsia measurement — 0.069 ink vs 0.055 solid, 'the ink separates ~25% better' — does not reproduce in ANY variant (I get ink 0.035 vs solid 0.040, the opposite ORDERING), and its `chart-finish: survey` recommendation for print and a11y decks rests entirely on it. Worse, it DELETED a correct finding: 'The texture channel needs no work… There is no six-wide cycle and no 7/8-wears-1/2 merge. An earlier draft claimed both; it was wrong' — it looked at the twelve-wide universal --cat-N-texture channel instead of the CHART channel, which I verified is six-wide (6n cycles, data-cat 0..5, data-series 0..5) against an eight-slot palette. So it ships an a11y section asserting nothing needs fixing where a real silent merge exists. Structurally, `folio` and `spread` share their mark treatment ENTIRELY (both 'filled', identical), so on any member with no furniture they lean on the frame alone; and `spread` degrades to inset-`folio` under print and on all five colour-free surfaces, which it states plainly — honest, but a failure of 'distinguishable on all 21'. Three refutations including a wrong palette-family count (14, not 15).

## Recommendation, and the grafts

**Winner: Track 3 — `pigment` / `etching` / `ground`.** It is the only track whose architecture survives the mechanism that decides the whole brief, and it has the most universal separation lever.

I verified the decisive fact in Chromium: a role token declared on the chart frame that references a slot-scoped name (`--chart-ink-value: var(--mark-ink)` on `[data-charts]`) computes to **black** — substitution happens where the declaration sits, so it resolves `--mark-ink` on the frame, where it does not exist — while the same reference inside a rule gated to the slot scope resolves correctly. Track 3 found this itself, named the six of its own declarations it killed, restructured (levels stay custom properties; inks become gated rules naming only role classes, so the zero-member-names target survives) and re-costed upward. Track 1 and Track 4 both ship blocks in the broken shape: Track 1 loses `--chart-edge-source` and `--chart-ink-value`, which are two of the three levers `ink` and `ground` differ on, so its design does not produce itself; Track 4 loses the TYPE half of `ledger`'s named carrier. Track 2 avoided the trap structurally but under-specifies its own type-ink lever.

Track 3 also has the best answer to the brief's stated central difficulty. Its finishes separate on **mark body depth** (82 / 30 / 50), present on 20 of 21 members and needing no gradient, no furniture and no frame — and the 82→50 drop on `ground` exists specifically so `ground` cannot ride on its panel alone, which is exactly how `finishes.spec.js`'s `plate` failed. Rule O′ is the only recognition anywhere that a per-mark ground **composites**, reintroducing radar's fourth-colour defect on a new register, answered with one clipped ground per group. And it specifies `buildGround` per substrate rather than deferring it, because deferring it is the `plate` failure a third time.

Three of its findings should be adopted regardless of which design ships: 19 of 21 members' fills are re-pointable with **zero transform edits** (a CSS rule beats the `fill="url(#…)"` presentation attribute; only pie and quadrant use inline `style`), with dead gradient defs as the consequence in exported bytes; the bearing cap of 52%/62% is AA-clean for `--text-heading` and `--cat-on-fill` and **catastrophic** for the four other inks the bearing members actually print, so the text gets re-pointed rather than the cap lowered; and the chrome ladder is **unbuilt** — all four tokens exist only in the failed spec, so no track may claim it as leverage.

**Graft into the winner before it goes to the trio.**

*From Track 4 (the strongest runner-up, and the source of the most important grafts):*
1. **The Layer A / Layer B split.** Track 4 is right that a `pigment` which re-groups members and colours every name in 21 cannot simultaneously be a no-op default. Land the correctness layer unregistered first; then the register, with `pigment` as a genuine no-op. This also fixes the winner's weakest framing.
2. **Texture is SVG-only.** Verified: all 33 a11y chart arms are `fill: url(#…)` and zero HTML members are textured. Track 3's Rule A is *unsatisfiable* on kanban, matrix-grid, progress, roadmap and timeline-list as written — and Track 3's own Rule G newly puts a categorical hue on matrix-grid's HTML cells, which makes this load-bearing. Take Track 4's two-arm split: texture/dash for SVG, a **value step** (plus the `--shape-*` mask) for HTML.
3. **Widen all four cycles together, and gate the rendered repeat period.** Verified: the chart texture and dash channels are six-wide (`6n`, `data-cat="0..5"`, `data-series="0..5"`) while pie and funnel cycle *colour* at `6n` too. Widening only the redundant channel manufactures a distinction not in the data. Track 3's "channel width == categorical cycle width" assertion needs Track 4's mechanism and its measurement (rendered period, not token count — a token-count assertion reads 8 == 8 and passes over the bug).
4. **Class token, not `[data-charts]`, and `:is(section, figure)`.** All fourteen registers stamp a class (`corners-rounded`); `data-corners` exists nowhere, and the class token is what makes the per-slide `_class:` override work. And `figure.chart-frame` is a real re-host — a `section`-only selector renders only the default in the Studio and docs previews.
5. **The instance-keyed last-channel guard** — "a finish may move the redundant channel, never remove the last one" — keyed on the transform's emitted mark count, because whether a direct label or an emblem *fits* is per-instance (a four-slice pie has room, an eleven-slice pie does not) and no static per-member attribute can carry it.

*From Track 1:*
6. **The magnitude × strength clause — the biggest hole in the winner.** `map`, `journey`, `matrix-grid` and `progress` encode with ramps (progress's is `20% + pct * 0.52`, coupled to `--pct`). A single global body depth either flattens those ramps, destroying the encoding, or is ignored by them, leaving `etching` and `pigment` identical on four more members. A ramp declares `--chart-ramp-lo/-hi` and a finish scales both endpoints by the factor its depth implies. Track 1 is the only track that closed this, and it lands squarely on Track 3's universal lever.
7. **The a11y clamp on body depth.** Track 3's own numbers say body30 clears the 3:1 floor in 0 of 224; Track 1 measured the same collapse on the grey ramp (8/8 slots under 3:1 at 26%). Where the texture override does not reach, the depth must resolve to `pigment`'s value. This is cheaper than Track 3's full a11y restructure and should ship as the interim answer while that restructure is sequenced.
8. **The one-line key fix** — append the slot attribute to the sibling `<text>` in `svg-legend.js`; one line serves every keyed member, and it collapses the confirmed base drift (`stacked-bar` mixes into `--chart-cat-base`, pie/funnel/map/quadrant into `--bg` — two different colours on dark).
9. **Keep Track 1's two-name fallback on the table** as the documented retreat if the render kills `etching`.

*From Track 2:*
10. **`chart-finish:` rather than `charts:`** — `resolve-finish.js` already owns a deck-level `finish:` register; an author who read "chart finishes" will type the colliding word.
11. **`vector-effect: non-scaling-stroke`** on `etching`'s doubled edge — verified that `stroke-width` is in viewBox units across boxes of 320×180, 420×348 and 300×300, so one multiplier renders at three physical weights. Track 3 identifies the problem and leaves the fix implicit.
12. **Rule S1's minimum drawn dimension** — a centred outline consumes the body it bounds on a thin waterfall delta or a small stacked segment; below a floor, fall back to the filled body.
13. **The figure inset** — a shape difference that survives monochrome, worth having on any finish whose ground is deleted in print.

**Corrections the winner must absorb, from the fact-check.** Radar **is** measurable — the separation tool composites `stop-opacity` now, candidate 5's claim is stale (Track 2 caught this; Track 4 wrongly withdrew a valid finding on it), and radar is still SWAMPED at 0.023/0.052. Do not carry Track 2's achromatopsia tier ordering forward: it does not reproduce and points the opposite way. Use 33, not 46, for the a11y `!important` arms, and 14, not 15, palette families.

**Before the trio hardens this, one decision is the human's** — Track 3 names it correctly and should not decide it alone: does the a11y/print restructure land *inside* this work or *gate* it? As the tree stands, 33 member-named `!important` arms override every finish, so all three would render identically on the five colour-free surfaces. And per HARD RULE #23, nothing in any of the four tracks has been rendered; `etching`'s central claim — that ink at stroke scale reads as strongly as a body — is unfalsified, and Track 3 is right to make one rasterized slide a precondition of adopting it rather than a follow-up.
