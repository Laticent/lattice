# Judged ranking — chart design language

Six tracks, 20 agents, 3.08M tokens, 0 errors. Candidates in `candidates/`.

## Track 5 — leverage-existing · 9/10

WINNER. Best spine, cleanest grounding, cheapest layer. Its one substantive strike is the 'eight cartesian members' error inherited from the brief itself (there are seven); everything else in a very large ledger came back confirmed, including seven line-exact 82%-mix locations and both worst-case theme/slot identifications. Two things put it first. (1) R0 — 'the register of a data mark is decided by one question: does the mark carry text inside it?' — is the single cleanest data-shape rule any track produced. It decides fill finish, text-on-fill safety, the quadrant inversion, and the bar/stacked-bar pair the brief singles out ('if the language fixes nothing else, it has to fix this pair') from ONE test, without naming a member. Every other track needs three or four rules to reach the same place. (2) It is grounded in the strongest single empirical finding in the competition: --text-heading on the 82% MARK level fails AA in 117 of 224 theme x mode x slot combinations (worst onyx/dark cat4 2.27:1) while both BACKDROP levels fail in 0/224 and 1/224 — re-derived exact by the fact-checker, worst cases included. That measurement turns 'text never sits on a mark' from three scattered source comments into a law. It also carries the family's answer to the exception (progress's plate, measured at 145/224 failing, worst 1.48:1, and getting away with it by laying a 16%/22% plate). It is the only track to RATIFY the legend face split rather than 'fix' it, with a real argument (T1: a key entry wears the register of the thing it names; the four Outfit legends are status keys naming pills set in --pill-font, the four JetBrains legends name categorical swatches) — every other track calls it an accident nobody chose. Its Q8 discipline is the best in the set: one new percentage constant plus eight derived aliases, four kernel lines, two deletions, and it names the real bug the token closes (the same '82% solid' resolves to two different colors on dark because funnel/map/pie-swatch mix toward --bg while stacked-bar and the domes mix toward --chart-cat-base). It also names a gate that CANNOT be built (extending TEXT_ON_FILL to the solid would fail 117/224 on day one; the correct check is structural, not contrast) — nobody else noticed that the obvious gate is a trap. Weaknesses, and they are real: the gradient section is the weakest part of the strongest document — it kills the dome and keeps the wash as BACKDROP material but never scores the alternatives, and R0 forcing bar to flatten is its biggest visual cost bought on rule-coherence rather than on a measured defect. It says so and hands it back as open question 1, which is the right posture. Motion (M0-M4) and reveal (D1-D2) are correct — it independently found the isRole five-vs-seven bug and line's D2 byte-neutrality guard — but shallower than the tracks that specialized there.

## Track 6 — clean-slate · 8.5/10

Best answer to the question the human flagged as sharpest, and the best cost presentation. One strike, inherited from the brief and self-contradicted in its own parenthesis. Three contributions are irreplaceable. (1) It is the only track that SCORES the gradient decision rather than asserting it: three options (keep both / the one-wash-one-edge variant chart-family.style.md already prototyped and held / flat) on the same four axes, landing on 'flat wins on coherence and reference practice, NOT on a demonstrated reader defect, and nobody should authorize it believing otherwise.' That is exactly the real thought the brief asked for, and it is the only place the held variant gets scored at all. (2) The load-bearing dependency nobody else drew: SVG fill cannot take a CSS gradient, so pie and quadrant emit inline style='fill:url()' which outranks any non-!important rule — so the dome is what blocks the universal texture channel, and the 45 + 42 !important rules (counted exactly) are its symptom, not accessibility's. That is the best argument for the dome verdict anywhere in the set. (3) Two genuine self-corrections against its own prior draft: it found that chart-mark-separation's paintOf never reads the ink edge on a washed bar (falls back to stroke only when fill is none — confirmed), patched an edge arm, and overturned its own measured case against the wash; and it withdrew its 'tall bar's top differs from a short bar's top' argument after confirming buildFillDefs uses the objectBoundingBox default. It also reported the data-anima-role counts correctly where Track 3 doubled them, read the 21 manifests for the CURRENT 14/5/2 render split where every other track quotes the stale 7/2/4 docs prose, dropped its own shape-cycle proposal on mechanism grounds, scoped map out explicitly rather than costing a 13-theme sequential palette at zero, and shipped a committed fixture because a different grouped-bar deck gives different magnitudes (the fact-checker calls that the most careful measurement note in the set). Why not first: its Q2 is the weakest type answer here — five roles collapsing category/series/legend/heading into one 'name', when the census measures eight and Tracks 3 and 5 both ratify eight with argument, and the collapse immediately needs a direct-label exception re-added. It over-flattens progress (Test B applied without the 'the ramp is a function of the datum' carve-out that four other tracks defend and chart-family.css documents approvingly). And it is the most expensive program on the table — 16 primitives plus an 11-slot channel, 76 decks, five export sign-off rounds — against CLAUDE.md's cheapest-path-that-meets-the-bar rule.

## Track 4 — accessibility-print · 8/10

The most original verified measurement of any track, and the best Q7 answer. One strike (the retired radar tick census artifact). Everything else it computed came back exact: cuoio's wash self-range 0.074-0.114 against the dome's 0.165-0.255, adjacent separation 0.017-0.095 at the wash midpoint, the flat-mix table (24/29/58/82% -> 0.053/0.063/0.127/0.179 separation and 10.64/9.61/4.85/2.58:1 text-on-fill), the five status-fill self-ranges with warn/info at 0.006 and info/mute at 0.000, the radar alpha composite, plus new decks built and run across three themes and a print-band gallery. Three things worth taking. (1) The Redundancy Ladder — position / direct label / value / shape-and-line-style / texture, with texture LAST — reframes 'six members have no non-colour channel' into 'four of the six already carry a stronger channel the tool cannot score, one needs colour taken away, and exactly one (map highlight) genuinely needs a new channel.' That is a re-diagnosis that halves the work, and every adjudication in it checks out against source. (2) The two rung preconditions nobody else states: rung 3 (value) is unavailable on a value-led palette because on onyx value IS the colour channel, so a member assigned it has one channel wearing two names; rung 5 (texture) is unavailable below ~16 viewBox units because the tile is hardcoded 8x8 userSpaceOnUse and cannot be tokenized. (3) The methodological discipline: it states the metric's worst-case-pairing assumption BEFORE using it, says the dome verdicts stand and the wash verdicts need two further arguments, labels every wash row worst-case, and closes with a seven-item UNVERIFIED section (PPTX rung-5 rendering as the largest exposure, currentColor inside a page-level pattern on any engine, the matched-pairing mode that does not exist, the other twelve themes). It also found a live WCAG AA failure — stacked-bar's hard-coded 82% literal at 2.58:1 on cuoio light, invisible to chart-contrast.test.js because an in-member literal is not the token the gate scores — and correctly filed it as pre-existing and off-path under HARD RULE #18 rather than folding it in. Why not first: Q5 is explicitly answered 'at lower altitude' and self-flagged as unprototyped and unmeasured, which is honest but leaves an eighth of the brief thin; and it asserts the wash verdict on a comparison (0.074 self-range vs the 0.06 gate floor) that Track 6's edge measurement directly contests.

## Track 3 — motion-designer · 7/10

The deepest motion answer in the competition by a wide margin, and the only track that gets the type-role count right. Its per-member census handle table — 21 rows of marks/anima/svgs — reproduced EXACTLY and the fact-checker calls it the most precisely reproduced table of the six. On Q5 it is the built answer where everyone else has a sketch: it found that hasAnimatableChart ('svg [data-mark]') is stricter than chartToScene's own candidate set and decides eligibility on all three code paths, so line animates only when an author happens to write a detail bullet (motion coupled to an unrelated authoring choice) while word-cloud and journey cannot be selected at all; that line emits 'line' and 'area' roles isRole rejects, so they animate as bars through the ?? 'bar' tail; that the frame must be declared on the ROLE and not inferred from data-cat, showing that inference breaks in both directions (legend swatches carry data-cat and would animate as figure; radar's polygons carry neither and would drop out, and chart-anima.ts already explains why they cannot take data-mark); and that the current timing arithmetic has its sign backwards — onset is buildSpan/n, so three marks arrive 704ms apart and eight arrive 405ms apart, producing a queue rather than a build. It then routes the timing fix back to the human under CLAUDE.md's second filter because it changes numbers the human set. Its 'legible' projection table is the first place reduce-not-remove is made true rather than asserted (three sources disagree today and the one that wins mounts the final frame). Its fill verdict is also sharper than most: the census's 'radial-gradient' bucket conflates a lightness ramp with an alpha ramp, and its third answer for radar — flatten the ramp, buy the rim emphasis back on the stroke where it survives greyscale and print — is elegantly argued. Why it does not place higher: one strike is a fabricated measurement, not a slip. It claimed a repo-wide scan of data-anima-role values and reported 50/38/24/22/4/4/4 when the real counts are exactly half (25/19/12/11/2/2/2, as Track 6 reported); that number reproduces under no scope. And six of the eight questions are answered at ordinary depth — this is a Q5 document with seven competent neighbours.

## Track 2 — ui-ux-interaction · 7/10

The sharpest read of the brief's methodology and the only product-grade answer to Q6, undercut by the weakest grounding among the mid-tier. Its best idea is meta: the shape is DERIVED per chart instance from the parsed model, not assigned per member, and it names the derived inputs (seriesCount, categoryCount, valuesPrinted, rampAuthored, markKind, markArea). It explicitly calls out that a static per-member shape table resolves every downstream rule by member name with a shape column in between — which is the failure mode the brief warned against and which four other tracks partly commit. On Q6 and the affordance layer it found things nobody else looked for, all confirmed: interactive() requires the .chart-details wrapper, so a chart where nobody authored a detail bullet is completely inert while an identical chart on the next slide is fully hoverable; liftAndTilt bails on animated charts, so motion:on silently removes the dim; the tilt is excluded for gantt by name and for the inline state-chart variant, and never fires on HTML marks; keyboard reveal is bound to 1-9 only and there is no tabindex anywhere in the chart bucket or the reveal layer; gantt emits its key inside an aria-hidden subtree; CHART_SVG_SEL is a hand-maintained 15-selector roster the skill doc already lists as silent-failure surface; and the exported --player carries motion but not reveal. Its keyboard model is checked against PRESENT_KEYMAP before claiming any verb — and it catches its own earlier draft binding arrow keys the transport owns. It also resolves the role='img' subtree-pruning conflict properly by putting keyboard reach on the key rail (DOM outside the pruned subtree) rather than citing both halves and moving on, and it corrects its own 'one edit to svg-legend.js' claim by counting the actual callers (seven on the shared builder, five self-keying). Why it sits here: five strikes, three of them the same census artifact the brief explicitly records as fixed and non-existent, plus a real one (chart-contrast.test.js does not resolve the deep gradient stop — that phrase belongs to a manual procedure in the style doc). And on the question the human called sharpest it withdraws rather than decides, leaving the wash verdict to a measurement it does not run.

## Track 1 — visual-designer · 6.5/10

Right gradient verdict, only track with rendered evidence, weakest grounding of the serious contenders. Its strengths are real: it is the only track that actually LOOKED — rasterizing gallery pages and confirming the domed wedges, the flat-and-divergent dark key swatches, radar's already-flat wash, and the desaturated dark funnel bands, which is HARD RULE #23 verification of the visual surface a visual-design language is about. Its churn counts are the most reproducible in the set (41 decks / 31 PDFs for the pie-quadrant union, exact), and its defence of the vertical wash is the best-sourced anywhere: bar.transform.js and line.transform.js already rotate the gradient so it shades thickness and never length, under comments that state the argument verbatim, so 'the argument for killing the wash is an argument its authors implemented.' It is also the only track that gets the cartesian-member count right (seven, where the brief itself says eight and four tracks repeat it), and its waterfall finding — the one member still ramping along the measured axis, fixed by rotating one argument — is a genuinely cheap, correct fix. The Loudness Law's insight that the failure mode is AREA, not brightness, and that a reference region is last because it is largest, is a good frame for the quadrant. But four strikes, and two are structural: its entire section 2 asserts three census detector fixes as cost items — the radar tick split, the missing .cart-zero pattern, the missing quadrant furniture classes — and all three are defects the BRIEF ITSELF records as non-existent and already fixed, crediting the visual-designer track with finding that they do not exist. Reasserting them means the primary source it was told to read first was not absorbed, and it puts three phantom work items in the cost section of a document whose job is to state cost. Add a 3.7x type-size ratio that reproduces under no reading of the viewBox math (the real figures are 1.11x, 1.64x or 2.15x) and a radar alpha integral that is wrong in the second and third terms (0.128 not 0.115; ~25% not 35-40%), and the document needs re-verification before anything is built on it.

## Recommendation

**Winner: Track 5 — "Three Registers".** It has the best spine, the cleanest fact-check record, the strongest single measurement, and the smallest spec layer — which is what the brief asked for ("a spec layer ABOVE the palette that every member implements", not a redesign).

Why it wins on the merits, not on its angle:

- **R0 is the best data-shape rule anyone produced.** "The register of a data mark is decided by one question: does the mark carry text inside it?" One test decides fill finish, text-on-fill safety, the quadrant's figure/ground inversion, and the bar/stacked-bar pair the brief singles out as the must-fix exhibit — without naming a member. Every other track needs three or four rules to reach the same place, and Track 2 is right that a static per-member shape table is member-keying in disguise; R0 is the one rule that cannot be.
- **It is grounded in the strongest empirical finding in the set.** `--text-heading` on the 82% MARK level fails AA in **117 of 224** theme×mode×slot combinations (worst 2.27:1), against **0/224** and **1/224** on the two BACKDROP levels — re-derived exact, worst cases included. That converts three scattered source comments into a law, and it comes with the family's own exception already built (progress measures 145/224 failing at worst 1.48:1 and survives only by laying a 16%/22% plate).
- **It ratifies rather than churns where ratifying is right.** It is the only track to defend the legend face split (a key entry wears the register of the thing it names — the four Outfit keys are status keys naming pills set in `--pill-font`; the four JetBrains keys name categorical swatches). Everyone else calls it an accident. It also names four things the tree already implements correctly (bullet = three registers on one chart, progress = the plate, line = the byte-neutral detail guard, svg-legend = both rail placements).
- **Q8 discipline and honest limits.** One new percentage constant plus eight derived aliases, four kernel lines, two deletions — and it names the base-divergence bug the token closes (funnel/map/pie-swatch mix toward `--bg` while stacked-bar and the domes mix toward `--chart-cat-base`, so the same "82% solid" renders two colours on dark). It is also the only track to identify a gate that *cannot* be built: extending `TEXT_ON_FILL` to the solid would fail 117/224 on day one, so the correct check is structural, not contrast.

**The one synthesis neither track states, and the winner must carry it.** Track 5's R0 sends bar to 82% flat on rule-coherence and flags that as its biggest unjustified cost. Track 4's measured flat-strength table answers it: **82% flat separates ~3× better than today's 24% tint** (0.179 vs 0.053 unsimulated; 0.102 vs 0.030 achromatopsic). The wash's replacement is not "flat at the same weight" — it is flat at a deeper weight that measurably buys separation, on a mark where R2 guarantees no text sits. That closes Track 5's open question 1 with a number instead of a preference, and it is the strongest available answer to the human's gradient question. Combined with Track 6's dependency argument, the family-wide verdict lands as: **dome dies (separation + it is what blocks the texture channel); the wash survives only where text rides the mark (gantt, kanban, timeline-list, state-chart — 223/224 clear AA there); text-free data marks go deeper and flat; radar keeps its translucency and loses its ramp; progress keeps its bound `--pct` ramp; waterfall rotates.**

### Grafts, in priority order

**From Track 4 (largest graft):**
1. The **flat-strength measurement table** (24/29/58/82% → separation and text-on-fill). It is the number that validates R0's outcome for bar and must ship in the winner's fill section.
2. **Split the solid into two strengths** — `-labeled` (AA-bound with headroom, ~5.5:1 target) and `-bare` (no text constraint, deeper). Track 5's single `--chart-fill-solid` is contradicted by funnel's deliberate 82%-with-labels-on-the-canvas, and this also settles Track 5's open question 3 (bullet's 88% is a *bare* mark, not a second constant) — noting the fact-checker's find that bullet's transform docblock justifying 88% is **stale, retracted by its own stylesheet**.
3. The **Redundancy Ladder** with its two preconditions (rung 3 unavailable on value-led palettes; rung 5 unavailable below ~16 viewBox units). It is a better Q7 than Track 5's N-rules because it re-diagnoses four of the six "uncovered" members as already carrying a stronger channel, and one (`map highlight`) as the only genuine gap.
4. The **UNVERIFIED section** as a required closing — especially PPTX rung-5 rendering and `currentColor` inside a page-level `<pattern>`, neither of which any track tested.
5. File **stacked-bar's 2.58:1 AA failure** as a pre-existing off-path issue (HARD RULE #18), with the reason the gate misses it (an in-member 82% literal is not the token `chart-contrast.test.js` scores).

**From Track 6:**
6. The **three-option scoring** (keep both / the held one-wash-one-edge variant / flat) with its verdict stated honestly — flat wins on coherence and reference practice, not on a demonstrated reader defect. The human asked for real thought here; this is the only place the already-prototyped variant gets scored at all.
7. The **§1↔§7 dependency**: inline `style="fill:url()"` outranks any non-`!important` rule, so the dome is what blocks the universal texture channel and the 45+42 `!important` rules are its symptom. Best argument for the dome verdict anywhere — and note it applies *specifically* to pie and quadrant, not to the wash members.
8. **Fix the instrument before quoting it**: `paintOf` never reads the ink edge on a washed bar and ignores `stop-opacity` entirely (so radar's 0.000 self-range is arithmetically forced). An edge/composite arm is a prerequisite of any future fill verdict.
9. The **D/U benefit column** and the deferrable tail, plus the **committed fixture** requirement — measurements vary by deck, so every published number names its deck, theme and simulation.

**From Track 3:** the whole motion answer, wholesale. Track 5's M0–M4 is correct but thin; Track 3 has the built version — the frame/mark split **declared on the role** (it proves inference from `data-cat` breaks in both directions), the `hasAnimatableChart` ↔ `chartToScene` selector mismatch that makes line's motion depend on whether someone wrote a tooltip, the `legible` projection table that makes reduce-not-remove true for the first time, and the timing inversion (onset as the primitive) correctly routed back to the human because it re-values numbers the human set.

**From Track 1:** the source-quoted rotation defence (bar and line already implement the across-thickness rule), waterfall's one-argument fix, the rendered-evidence discipline, and its reproducible PDF-churn method.

### Corrections the hardened winner must absorb

- **"Eight cartesian members" is wrong — there are seven.** The error originates in the brief and Tracks 2, 3, 5 and 6 all repeat it; only Track 1 has it right.
- **Do not carry Track 1's §2.** All three "census detector fixes" are defects the brief explicitly records as non-existent and fixed; they are phantom cost items.
- **Keep progress's bound ramp** (Tracks 1/3/4/5 agree; Track 6 over-flattens by dropping the "the ramp is a function of the datum" carve-out that `chart-family.css` documents approvingly).
- **Eight type roles, not five** (Tracks 3 and 5 both ratify eight with argument; Track 6's collapse immediately needs an exception re-added).
- **matrix-grid's categorical cycle is eight slots wide, not six** — the brief and several tracks say six.
- Type roles fix face, ink, case and tracking but **not size**: sizes are viewBox user units and the boxes differ (cartesian 320×180, quadrant 420×348, radar 300×300), so quadrant adopts the axis-title *register* and keeps its own size. Track 1's "3.7×" figure for this reproduces under no reading and should not be quoted.
