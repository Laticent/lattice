01 / The correction

Sheet 01 derived a Frame's reframe set from three atoms — order, rank, arity — and reported, as a side result, that atelier, ruled, mount, column, overline and bleed all read before/thesis/1: the same reading as standard.

That result was the answer and I filed it as a footnote. A Frame that does not change the reading is not a Frame — it is a treatment, and Lattice already owns an axis for treatments.

Lattice's model is Function · Form · Substance · Finish, four axes, since design/design-system.md. Finish answers “what should it feel like?” and is surfaced through three registers: theme: (color), mode: (the typographic and geometric hand), finish: (the backdrop). Eleven of my sixteen were mode: values wearing a Frame's clothes.

Two were near-exact duplicates of shipped code. ruled is finish: ledger — “fine horizontal ruled lines + a bold left margin bar + a top-right corner fold.” mat is finish: gallery — “a museum inset keyline frame + a spotlight.” That is HARD RULE #15, and I broke it.

02 / The boundary, stated once

The two axes were colliding because nothing said where one stops. The three atoms give a test that is not a matter of taste.

Form owns the reading

order — is the thesis met before the evidence, after it, or not at all.
rank — what holds the reserved position.
arity — how many things the slide asserts.

Change any one and you have changed the argument. That is a Frame.

Finish owns everything else

Type, line, box, mark, measure, ornament. A treatment may make the same argument look austere, drawn, ruled or engineered.

It renders the same reading in a different hand. That is a Mode.

The test: does it change order, rank or arity? If no, it is not a Frame. Nine Frames pass it. Everything else in sheet 01 failed it, including four entries that are simply different carves of the same reading — a compressed masthead, a side column, a bled stage, a canvas well — which are Frame variants the way a component has variants.

math, and why the unary readings are five, not six

Sheet 01 gave math the reading before/none/1, which is not among the nine — so the collapse had nowhere to put it. The reading was wrong, and the reason generalizes.

rank: none is degenerate at arity 1. With one content cell, “no cell is privileged” and “the one cell is privileged” describe the same slide — there is nothing to withhold emphasis from. rank: none only carries meaning at arity ≥ 2, which is exactly where it works: compare is before/none/2, and there the none is a real stance — neither of these two is the answer, you weigh them.

So the unary readings are five and the asymmetry in the reading table is derived rather than arbitrary. What made math look sovereign is two things that are not the reading: it drives its own > h2 title grid, which is a carve; and it centers a display equation in the stage, which is the component owning its stage, as Law I grants. A frame-reserved position is a band the Frame holds beyond the stage — plinth’s takeaway, margin’s rail. Math’s equation is inside its own stage, so no rank is reserved. It is standard with a title-grid carve.

The logo does not do what the model says it does

logo.manifest.json declares fits: ["masthead-bay"] and forms.md §5.1 lists it as docked there. The render does something else — base.modifiers.css pins it at top: var(--frame-inset-y) / right: var(--frame-inset-x) as an absolutely positioned child of the section, and logo-x / logo-y set its center as a percentage of the whole slide. It sits on the content plane deliberately: on chrome an off-corner brand plate erased four words of a pull-quote; on atmosphere it vanished under a split panel.

Under one Frame that divergence is invisible. Under nine it generates bugs: logo-y: 82 means one thing when the masthead is on top and another when foot puts it at the bottom. The Tile has form here — 2026-08-04-finish-stacking-displaces-frame-chrome.md measured those coordinates silently ceasing to mean anything, rendering at 92.2% x on every logo slide in the corpus with y drifting 84 / 87.1 / 88 / 100 across four slides that all declared 82.

The contested corner

Top-right is the busiest 80×50px on the slide: the logo, the status stamp and (where a bay survives) the meta line all want it. The engine already arbitrates — --corner-logo-reserve stacks the state tabs to the logo’s left, because clearing it vertically needs ~80px of drop and would put a transient marker a third of the way into the body. That was a real defect: logo: plus confidential sliced the top off the mark. So each Frame owes a corner order, not just a list of homes.

foot splits the masthead, and that is the interesting row. The lede Cell moves to the bottom; the bay does not. A bay at the bottom would collide with pagination, and masthead-lede and masthead-bay are already two Cells in lib/forms/cell/, so moving one without the other costs nothing. It reads better too — the deck’s identity stays where the eye rests first, and only the argument moves.

bookend’s status is the open hole. A WIP or CONFIDENTIAL stamp on a title slide has nowhere to go — the frame silences the corner stack, and it is the slide in a deck most likely to need the marker. Third time this catalog has flagged it, which is a fair argument for making it the first Law II fix.

The same test confirms the pair it might have collapsed: bookend reserves the position and fills it with the thesis (none/thesis/1); quiet reserves nothing at all (none/none/1). Both coherent, genuinely distinct.

04 / The Mode contract

A Mode is a deck-wide hand every component honors. Lattice ships one real value — sketch — and its governing rule generalizes: roughen the lines the deck draws, never invent a box. Below is that rule written as a contract, so a second, third and sixth value stay cohesive instead of each becoming a bespoke skin.

Six channels — a Mode value must set every one

1

Type

Display and body families, weight, tracking. The single strongest carrier of a hand. sketch sets Caveat for headings and Shantell Sans for prose; a Mode that leaves type alone will read as the baseline with decoration on it.

2

Line

How a drawn rule renders — weight, and its mechanism. This is not cosmetic: sketch replaces CSS borders with real rough.js strokes on table frames, row rules, the list.principles rules, the <hr> and the masthead↔stage divider. A ruled Mode would want hairlines at a fixed rhythm; an engineered one, a two-weight stroke system.

3

Box

Corner geometry, fill posture (filled · outlined · none) and elevation, for every card-style layout. Nine components draw cards; they must all change together or the deck reads as two decks.

4

Mark

Bullets, ticks, numerals and the --mark-* / --shape-* SVG masks. HARD RULE #29 already forbids typing a shape glyph, so a Mode changes the mask, never the character.

5

Measure & rhythm

Text measure and vertical rhythm — how much air the deck keeps. This is the channel sheet 01's manuscript entry was really about, and the one most often forgotten, which is why a new hand can look right on a title slide and wrong on a fourteen-row table.

6

Ornament

Furniture the Mode may add — a folio, corner ticks, a title block, a registration mark — to frame chrome only, never to the stage. This is the clause that keeps ornament from becoming layout.

Seven clauses

ClauseRuleWhy, and what it protects
M1 Restyle, never restructureMay change how an existing surface renders. May not add or remove a box, Cell or slot.sketch's own rule, generalized. The moment a Mode adds a box it is deciding layout, and layout is Form.
M2 Reading-preservingMay not change order, rank or arity.The load-bearing clause. It is what makes Frame × Mode compose instead of collide, and it is testable rather than a matter of taste.
M3 Palette-blindNames no color. Where a structure carries meaning-bearing color — an actor hue, redline's add/remove spine — the Mode changes its geometry and never its hue.HARD RULE #3. Color is Theme. sketch already obeys this exactly: it wobbles a border's corners and leaves its color alone.
M4 Total coverageDefines every channel for every component that paints in it. No component silently falls back to the baseline hand.The cohesion the axis exists for. sketch itself fails this today, by its own docs — boxed blockquotes and bordered rows “still bend a border-radius; they convert next.”
M5 Content-blindNever changes what a component contains, nor its capacity budget.Otherwise it is Substance. A Mode that made a list hold four items instead of six would silently re-author decks.
M6 Export-safeSurvives the PDF and PPTX paths at full fidelity.The Finish layer already carries this scar: every gradient fades opaque-to-opaque, never to transparent, because Chromium's print path turns an alpha fade into a gray cloud the browser hides.
M7 EscapableOne slide returns to baseline.Already works — <!-- _class: boardroom --> opts a slide out of mode: sketch.

M4 is the only clause a machine can cheaply check, and it is the one that decides whether the axis is worth having. Render all 69 components under a Mode and assert none falls back to the baseline hand. Without that gate, a Mode ships at 40% coverage and every deck using it is visibly two decks — which is exactly the state sketch is in today.

05 / How many Modes — and why personas are the wrong unit

Fourteen teams do not need fourteen hands. A persona is not a style; a deliverable is. One security team ships an audit report that must read as checked and an architecture review that must read as engineered — two different Modes from one team, in the same quarter. Index the posture, not the org chart, and the space collapses.

The question a Mode answers is what must the audience believe about these claims? Six answers cover the listed teams. This is a hypothesis the contract above lets you test, not a committed catalog — the values come after the contract, and each one costs a full pass over 69 components.

Six is near the ceiling, and the cost argument is why. Modes multiply: nine Frames × six Modes × eighteen palettes is 972 combinations to keep coherent, and M4 prices each Mode at a full pass over all 69 components. Beyond six, the marginal hand is one nobody can tell from its neighbor and nobody can afford to keep complete.

The gap, stated plainly: nine backdrops ship and one hand does. finish: carries atrium · meridian · strata · halo · ledger · nimbus · loom · savile · gallery — atmosphere painted behind content, which touches no component's geometry. mode: carries sketch alone. Lattice has the weather for eight styles and the grammar for one.

06 / One naming collision to settle first

ledger already means two things: a finish: backdrop, and a component Form value (the shape actors, glossary, inventory and kpi take). A mode: ledger would be its third sense, across three different axes — which design-system.md §2.5 bans outright: one system word per concept, no third synonym. The posture is named Rigor above for that reason; the register value needs a word that is not already spent.
