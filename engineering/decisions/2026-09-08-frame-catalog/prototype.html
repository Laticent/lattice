The Frame Catalog

Lattice ships twelve Frames. Ten of them are sovereign, and every one of those ten is welded to a single component of the same name. The other fifty-nine components choose between one shape and the same shape with the progress rail switched off. This is a catalog of twenty-eight: the twelve that ship, plus sixteen that turn the Frame from a per-component escape hatch into a vocabulary a component picks from.

01 / The frame is not a choice yet

A Frame's job, in design/forms.md, is to carve the slide into Cells so a component can fill one of them. In practice the carving never varies. Every component that is not one of the ten sovereigns gets standard: a masthead band on top, a stage in the middle, a footer band below. minimal is the same frame with the progress rail dropped.

The ten sovereign Frames look like variety, but each is named after — and reachable only by — one component. split-panel the Frame exists for split-panel the component. A chart cannot ask for it. The binding is Set membership in masthead.transform.js, not a declared choice.

So the axis that decides where the title sits, how tall the stage is, and where a key insight lands has, for most of the catalog, exactly one value.

02 / How many frames can there realistically be?

The honest way to answer is to derive it, not to pick a round number. Four axes describe a Frame. Their raw product is 675. Most of that product is illegal, invisible, or already a knob elsewhere in Lattice.

AxisValuesWhat it variesSurvives?
Stage topology9full · banded · columned · mounted · bled · margined · dual · triple · posterYes — this is the frame
Masthead position5top band · left column · bottom · overlaid · absentYes, but only ~22 of the 45 pairs are legal
Chrome posture3full · no rail · silentNo — already a per-slide control (silent, no-progress)
Canvas treatment5flat · tinted · plated · bled · ruledPartly — 2 are the finish: / lift: registers; 3 change geometry

675 → drop chrome posture (it is orthogonal, and folding it in would triple every frame) → 225. → keep only legal topology × masthead pairs, 22 of 45 → ~110. → collapse the treatments that are really registers, keep the three that move a box → ~30 distinguishable Frames. Of those, two pairs read the same at ten feet and one has no component that can fill it.

Twenty-eight is the catalog. Twelve of them form a boardroom core that a bank deck never needs to leave. The ceiling is low because a 16:9 rectangle read for forty seconds cannot hold more than three regions before it stops being a slide and starts being a dashboard.

03 / Three laws that make 28 Frames hold 69 components

The reason today's sovereigns are welded to one component each is that they carve two content cells and only a component authored for two cells can fill them. These three rules are what let a Frame be picked rather than inherited.

  1. I

    One stage per component

    A Frame hands the component exactly one contiguous stage Cell. Every other Cell it carves is frame-owned — chrome, or an editorial band the Frame fills from parts the author already writes (meta, coda, stamp). A Frame that carves a second content cell is a different family, and admits only split-capable components. Ten of today's twelve break this; that is the whole defect.

  2. II

    Chrome is relocated, never dropped

    Title, running header, footer, pagination, rail, meta, coda and the state stamp each get a declared home in every Frame — even if that home is silent. A component with a Key Insight must not lose it by landing on a different Frame. Sovereign Frames today suppress seven Cells apiece with no re-home, which is why a coda vanishes on eight layouts.

  3. III

    The stage publishes an aspect band

    Each Frame declares the aspect ratio its stage resolves to at 16:9, and whether it admits flow bodies, canvas bodies, or both. A component already declares which it is. Admissibility becomes derivable — frame.admits ⊇ component.stage — instead of a hand-maintained Set. This is forms.md §7's accepts/fits pair finally doing work.

04 / The catalog

Each plate is drawn to true 16:9 at Lattice's real geometry — 64px stage inset, 88px top inset, a 104px footer reserve, the 30px edge berth the running header and footer share. Ghosted parts are chrome the Frame throws away today with nowhere to put it.

Not every Frame reframes

Twenty-eight shapes is not twenty-eight arguments. Measured against standard holding the same content, each Frame changes one of three things — and only one of the three is a reframe.

Six of the sixteen new Frames reframe, and a component can freely select all six. The clearest case is foot: the same chart and the same words, but in standard the title is a label read before the evidence, and in foot it is a conclusion read after it. Assertion-first becomes evidence-first with no edit to the markdown.

Reframing is Law II's dividend. Moving the title under the stage is a reframe rather than a mutilation only because every chrome part still has a declared home — the same authored parts, re-ranked. A Frame that could drop chrome would just lose the title.

05 / What it buys, per component

Admissibility is derived, not listed: a Frame admits a stage kind, a component declares one, and a short exclusion list handles the cases where a Frame's stage is too narrow or too tall for a particular body. The two scores are deliberately independent. manuscript is a 8 as a design and a 6 in a bank; plinth is the reverse of an artisan frame — unremarkable to look at, and the single most useful shape a consulting deck has.
