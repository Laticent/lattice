# Checks: one name and one owner for the six answers (2026-10-18)

Status: built. Owner decisions are in §12, earlier positions in §3, and what landed is in §15 to §19 (PR #2972). Parents: `2026-09-24-six-state-marks.md` (the six answers and
their one meaning each), `2026-10-17-pills-plugin.md` (PR #2755: the embedded pills plugin, the token rule in §3 and the
plugin classes in §4.5), `2026-08-25-typed-glyphs.md` (HARD RULE #29: we draw the shape), and
`2026-09-29-inline-icons.md` (the icons plugin).

## 1. The answer

1. **Name it what it is.** The six answers are the outcomes of a check: yes, partly, no, unknown, not yet, does not
   apply. The author-facing docs already call the family `checks-*` and the component `checklist`. We adopt **check** and
   retire "mark" and "state" for this sense (§5).
2. **One owner.** One piece owns the vocabulary (six rows), the six drawings, the `[x]` grammar, the words and narration,
   autocomplete and lint. Components and pills never re-implement any of it.
3. **Containment, not flags.** A check sits inside a container: a pill, a badge, a table cell, a list row. The container
   holds whatever it is handed and does not know what a check is. The pill plugin gains no check attribute (§6).
4. **Plugin or host-level is a later call.** Staging comes first: name, registry, composition proven on `verdict-grid`.
   Whether the owner becomes an embedded plugin is decided after that (§7).

**One idea, seven names.** The six answers are called a *marker* (the typed `[x]`), a *state* (`state-marks.js`, `.state`,
`--state-color`), a *mark* (`--mark-check`), a *disc* (the docs), a *check* (`checks-bold`, `checklist`), an *answer* (the
meaning), and a *badge* (the chip that wraps one). "State" also names a state-chart's finite-state-machine states.

**Who reads the checks.**

- Twelve stylesheets read the mask tokens: `checklist`, `obligation-matrix`, `verdict-grid`, `roadmap`, `state-chart`,
  `line`, `bar`, `stacked-bar`, `slope`, `hub-spoke`, `chart-family` and `base.modifiers.css`.
- Three transforms build a `.badge` span from a leading `[x]`: `verdictGridBadges` (which also covers `pricing`) and
  `obligationMatrixBadges` (which also covers the universal `state-cells` modifier) in
  `lib/integrations/markdown-it/plugins.js`, and a DOM mirror in `lib/runtime/index.js`. Each builds the same
  chip-with-a-check, by hand.
- The grammar is read by Segno's list-text grammar (`leading-marker.js`, `list-text-grammar.js`) for a leading `[x] Senior`
  in list items and cells, and by the host's `state` inline-code kind for `` `[x]` `` (`HOST_INLINE.kinds` lists `state`).
- Compose edits them (`docs/e2e/compose-state-markers.spec.ts`), and `lint-core.js` coaches authors toward them
  (the typed-glyph rule, HARD RULE #29).

**A second meaning vocabulary sits beside the six.** `lib/core/chart-status.js` holds ten status words (`on-track`, `done`,
`live`, `at-risk`, `warn`, `blocked`, `fail`, `pilot`, `decision`, `deferred`), painted by `.chart-status` in
`chart-family.styles.css` and written as pill-shaped records. They describe how a piece of work is going. The six checks
describe whether a criterion is met. `warn` and `fail` appear in both lists.

## 3. Owner positions, 2026-10-18

Recorded as stated. Nothing else in this note is decided.

- "Mark" means several things in Lattice. Call each what it is and find a better name for this one.
- A pill is not stuffed with another attribute. The owner holds to the Unix philosophy: small tools that each do one
  thing, and accept a higher cost up front for less confusion and room to evolve.
- A component does not write its own semantics and translate them into a pill. It builds on the plugin's semantics
  (`2026-10-17-pills-plugin.md`, §3).
- Checks get their own design note and their own line of work, separate from the pills PR (HARD RULE #17).

## 4. The model

Four primitives, each with one job:

| Primitive | Its one job | Vocabulary | Knows about |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pill** | A labeled container: shape, color slot, size, room for content | Open labels, 12 ordinal colors | Nothing about checks, statuses or icons |
| **Icon** | One drawn glyph, by name | Open (a library) | Nothing about pills |
| **Check** | One of six answers, drawn as a disc | Closed, six rows | Nothing about pills |
| **Status** | A health word for a piece of work | Closed, ten rows | Nothing about pills (§8) |

Five rules keep them apart:

1. **Meaning lives in a check or a status, never in a pill or an icon.** A pill's color slot picks contrast and never
   meaning, as the pill docs already say.
2. **Components read checks, never pill labels or colors.** `verdict-grid` accepts `[x]`, the shared concept.
3. **A component composes, it does not interpret.** It places one primitive inside another.
4. **The vocabulary is data.** A check row holds its id, its marker character, its meaning, its drawing, its tone token, its
   default words, its narration words and its autocomplete entry. Adding an answer is adding a row, with no new syntax and
   no per-component edit. Today six rows exist across `state-marks.js`, the label sets and the mask tokens; the work is
   to make them one.
5. **One parse.** `2026-09-24-six-state-marks.md` §6.1 already requires it (HARD RULE #1).

**An enforceable test of the Unix rule:** a unit test that fails if `lib/plugins/pills/pills.inline.js` mentions "check",
"status" or "mark". The pill's existing `icon=` word is the one known exception, and it is called out, not copied.

## 5. The name

| Candidate | For | Against |
|---|---|---|
| **check** | Already author-facing (`checks-bold`, `checks-outline`, `checklist`); CI vocabulary, where a check ends pass, fail, skipped | `npm run check` and `build:check` use the word for tooling, a different audience |
| state | The host's inline kind is already `state` | Collides with a state-chart's states and the `.state` classes |
| verdict | Natural for `verdict-grid` | `verdict-grid`'s verdict is the whole recommended card |
| status | Plain English | Collides with the ten chart statuses |
| answer | Says what the six are | Too generic to search for |
| mark(er) | The current name | Seven meanings already |

**Recommendation: `check`.** It matches what authors already read. The typed `[x]` stays as the bracket form of a check.
The final name is the owner's call. A rename lands in stages: docs and new code first, file and token names later, with the
old names kept as aliases until each importer has moved.

## 6. How a check goes inside a pill

| Option | How it works | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| A. A check word on the pill | `{Residency, check=pass}` | Rejected by the owner (§3). Each new meaning would add another flag. |
| B. Siblings | `` `[x]` `{Residency}` `` side by side | Works today. The check cannot sit inside the pill's edge, so the chip look is impossible. |
| **C. Containment** | The pill renders the content it is handed. A check draws itself inside. | Recommended. Needs the unknowns below answered. |
| D. The component owns the badge | Status quo: three components build their own | Rejected: it is the duplication this note exists to remove. |

With C, `verdict-grid` keeps `[x] Residency` as its authoring syntax, since that syntax is the shared concept and
`checklist` and `obligation-matrix` use it too. The component asks the pill renderer for a pill whose content is a check
followed by the text. The `.badge` geometry in `verdict-grid`, `pricing` and `obligation-matrix` is deleted, and the runtime
mirror shrinks to the same call.

**Unknowns, to answer with a spike before anything lands:**

- A pill's label with a `[` or `]` must be quoted today (`` `{"[x] Residency", tag}` ``), so an author writing the composed
  form directly gets an awkward spelling. Components compose it, so authors rarely write it, but a small Segno rule may be
  needed.
- Whether the pill's text path can run the leading-check reader without special cases in the pill.
- Whether narration, lint and Compose still read the composed result.

## 8. Statuses

yes; `deferred` is close to not yet. Nothing changes in `chart-status.js` or `.chart-status` now. The question is decided
after §6, because it decides whether `.chart-status` is the same job as a check.

## 16. Step 4 landed: the pill accepts content, 2026-10-29

The pill takes an optional `lead` from its caller and puts it before the label in a `.lat-pill-lead` span

The two defects §14 found are fixed here, ahead of step 5, so the migration cannot ship them:

**The check is drawn smaller inside a pill (2026-10-29, the owner's pick).** Measured in Chromium against a plain

## 18. Step 6 landed: the embedded checks plugin, 2026-10-29

`lib/plugins/checks/` is an embedded plugin (§12 decision 3), like pills: it loads on every render and has no off
switch. It owns the six answers end to end:

| What | Where |
|---|---|
| The registry (the six rows, and the statuses in §19) | `shared/registry.js`, moved from `lib/core/state-marks.js` |
| Composing a check into a pill or a row | `shared/compose.js`, moved from `lib/core/check-compose.js` |
| Reading a check that leads list or cell text | `shared/leading.js`, moved from `lib/core/leading-marker.js` |
| The bracket shortcut in inline code, `` `[x]` `` | `checks.inline.js`, kind `state` (the host's last first-party row) |
| The full form, `` `@check{…}` `` | `checks.inline.js`, kind `check`, named-only |
| The drawing: shape to mask, the hollow answers, the inline disc | `checks.styles.css`, moved from `base.modifiers.css` unchanged |
| Autocomplete and lint | `nextWords` and `diagnose`, wired into `lib/authoring/lint-core.js` |

**The full form** (§12 decisions 2, 5 and 7; spellings confirmed by the owner, 2026-10-29). The answer comes first
and is one of the six ids (`pass warn fail unknown todo skip`), which are canonical, or the spoken word the registry's
`aliases` column lists (`yes partly no open na`). The label is `label=`, a named Segno parameter, so it is quoted like
any Segno text and never collides with the bracket. Without a label the check is the disc alone; with one, the same pill
verdict-grid composes. A bare `{pass}` stays a pill: the full form needs its name.

**A new manifest declaration, `shortcut`.** The resolver knew two ways to reach an inline kind: a tag character
before a record, or the bare record (`untagged`, the pill's). The bracket shortcut is neither, because it is not a
record. `contributes.inline.<kind>.shortcut` names the one character such a kind opens with. Only an embedded plugin may
declare one; a character is claimed once; it is one ASCII punctuation character; and `{`, `@`, the Segno tags, the
escape `\` and a backtick are refused, as is a kind that is both a shortcut and untagged (`lib/plugins/resolve.js`,
`plugin.schema.json`, pinned in `test/unit/plugins/contribution-points.test.js`). The red-team review found the first
version accepted `\` (which would have stopped `\[x]` escaping) and `é` (which the dispatcher refuses at load, taking
every render path down). **The cost to name:** the checks plugin now owns the `[` lead outright, so a later inline form
that opens with `[` (a footnote `[^1]`, a citation `[@key]`) has to route through the checks row or take the
character from it. The host's own `state` row is gone, so `HOST_INLINE.kinds` is empty and `FIRST_PARTY` is an
empty list. `state` and `check` joined `pill` as kind names only an embedded plugin may declare.

**The grammar stays shared, and reads the registry.** The Segno list-text grammar (`lib/core/list-text-grammar.js`)
reads matrix-grid's positional cells and the `_track` directive as well as the leading check, so moving it into the
checks plugin would make two other features depend on checks for grammar that is not a check. It stays in `lib/core`,
and its check marker set is now read from the registry rather than retyped, so a seventh answer reaches the parser
through `npm run segno-lib:build` with no edit there. The reader that walks it for a check moved into the plugin.

**The boundary is one-way, and it runs from core into the plugin.** Thirteen engine files outside the plugin require
`lib/plugins/checks/shared/` (among them `lib/core/chart-status.js`, `lib/core/status-marks.js` and
`lib/core/list-text-grammar.js`), so the chart statuses and the core grammar now depend on an embedded plugin. Under
§12 decision 3 (embedded, no off switch) that costs nothing today. It does close a door on purpose: checks can never be
made switchable, or lifted out of the tree, without first moving the registry back to core. The pill, by contrast,
knows nothing of checks (the Unix test below), so the dependency is never circular.

**Narration reads a check as the item's answer only when it leads.** The red-team review found the first version
read any check that was an item's first ELEMENT as its answer, and `:first-child` skips text: `` Encryption `[!]` at
rest `` was spoken "Encryption at rest: no", a verdict the slide does not draw. `leadCheckOf` now also asks that only
blank text comes before the check (`test/unit/transformers/prose-projection.test.js`).

**Retired.** The core Segno `state` slot (`lib/core/segno-slots.js`), which spelled the six as
`done partial fail unknown todo skip`, a third vocabulary that nothing read. Its one useful idea, the brackets as
Segno shortcuts for the record, moved into the plugin's slot, so a spelling reader sees `[x]` and `@check{pass}` as one
value.

**Lint parity.** A criterion moved from `[x] Residency in the EU` to `@check{pass, label="Residency in the EU"}` used to
drop out of its density budget, because the word count drops inline code (measured: the same verdict-grid lost its
`density-overflow` hint). `lib/authoring/prose-budgets.js` now counts the full form in its shortcut spelling, so the
two forms count the same by construction.

**One trap, warned rather than fixed.** The label a composed check sits beside is the item's plain text, on both render
paths, as the `.badge` label was on main: `**bold**` loses its weight and inline code prints its source. That is old,
but `@check` is a spelling this step teaches, and `` [x] `@check{fail}` text `` showing "@check{fail} text" is a trap
it set. `lint:deck` now warns on it (`check-label-code`, `spec/diagnostics.md`); it fires on no tracked deck. Keeping
rich labels would mean composing inline tokens rather than text on both paths, which is a separate change.

**The Unix test** (§4, §10) holds the pill to knowing nothing of checks. A word grep cannot say that (`markup`,
`--cat-N-mark` and `checkedParts` are fine), so the test reads the pill's code with comments stripped for an import of
the checks plugin or a name only a check has. `RESERVED_MARKERS` is the stated exception: it keeps `{x}` from reading as
a pill, a grammar collision guard and not a check.

**Evidence.** The engine render of all 482 tracked decks is byte-identical to step 5, and the 45 pages of the
checklist, pricing, obligation-matrix, state-marks and style-variant decks are pixel-identical: the move changed no
output. The plugin's 11 conformance cases run in the harness; the Studio's completion offers the six answers with
their meanings, then `label=`.

## 19. Step 7 landed: the statuses join the registry, 2026-10-29

§12 decision 4: one shape, two sets. `shared/registry.js` holds the six checks as `answers` and the ten chart
statuses as `progress`. Every row of both carries `id`, `word` and `mark`, the mark named from one vocabulary,
`DRAWINGS` (`check dash x question slash dot`, each a `--mark-*` token); each set adds what only it needs. A check row
names its bracket marker, shape class, ink token and meaning; a status row names the role it is painted in
(`pass warn fail info mute`). `lib/core/chart-status.js` (the legend order, the spoken words) and
`lib/core/status-marks.js` (the mark per status) now read the table. The chart family's stylesheet cannot import it,
so `test/unit/core/state-marks.test.js` holds its mark and hue per status to the rows.

**The overlap §8 asked to test, measured from the drawings.** A status names the answer whose drawing it shares
(`sharesDrawingWith`), and the test fails if it names any other:

| Status | Draws | Shared with |
|---|---|---|
| on-track, done | check | yes |
| at-risk, warn | dash | partly |
| blocked, fail | cross | no |
| deferred | slash | does not apply |
| live, pilot, decision | dot | none |

One finding is worth an owner's look later, and nothing in this PR acts on it: `deferred` draws the slash of "does not
apply", while in meaning it is closer to "open, not yet". The drawing stays as shipped; the table records the tension.

**Nothing renders differently.** The ten words, their order and their marks are the ones that shipped; the test pins the
legend order and two spoken words outright.

## 20. The marks are drawn from Tabler, 2026-10-29

The six status marks (`--mark-check`, `-dash`, `-x`, `-slash`, `-question` and the bold set with `-dot-bold`) were our
own hand-drawn SVG paths. They now come from the same pinned Tabler Icons package the icons plugin draws from
(HARD RULE #15: reuse before you draw). The owner asked whether icons could replace the hand-rolled marks and keep every
look and variant; this section is the answer, and the change.

**Every variant survives because the variants never touch the mark.** `checks-knockout`, `-ringed`, `-bold`,
`-outline` and `-tonal` restyle the disc, its ring and its fill. The mark is a CSS mask the disc's color shows through,
so any single-color drawing works there. What does matter is the mark's size and weight inside the disc.

**Tabler's set, as curated for the icons plugin, has none of them.** Its 265 icons are picked for architecture and
business slides; `check`, `minus`, `x`, `slash`, `question-mark` and the filled `point` are in the package but not in
`curation.json`, and adding them there would publish six new author-facing icon names nobody asked for. So the marks
are their own list, `lib/plugins/_icons-source/marks.json`, read by the same generator
(`tools/build-icons-data.js`), which validates each Tabler node exactly as it validates an icon and writes a
`BEGIN GENERATED STATUS MARKS` block into `lib/base/base.tokens.css`; `--check` (and so `build:check`) fails on a hand
edit.

**The fit is baked into the coordinates, and the charts get the same paths.** The checker on this change found two
defects in the first version, which wrapped each Tabler path in a `<g transform>`. The PPTX/ODP exporter lifts a mark
as a native shape only when the mask is a bare `<svg><path/></svg>` (`docs/src/lib/calco/reader.ts` `markOf`), so every
mark and its pill fell into a raster picture; and the SVG charts (gantt, hub-spoke, risk-matrix, flowchart,
state-chart) carry their own copy of the bold paths (`MARK_PATH` in `lib/core/status-marks.js`, copied into the
flowchart and state-chart layout passes), which still drew the old marks while HTML charts drew Tabler's. The generator
now applies the fit to the path's own numbers (every command made absolute), and writes `MARK_PATH` into all three
files between `BEGIN/END GENERATED MARK PATHS` markers. The bold slash is 3.8 everywhere; the charts' copy had 3.6, a
mismatch older than this change. A chart draws the dot as a 7-unit round point (a zero-length stroke), the same disc the
token fills, because `.chart-status-mark` strokes every mark. `test/unit/plugins/status-marks-tabler.test.js` pins each
mask to a bare path in its Tabler icon's commands and the charts' table to the bold tokens; the exporter's refusal
budgets (`calco-refusals.test.js`) pass on the rebuilt bundle. Baked, the check is `M6 12.1L10 16.1L18 8.1`, half a unit
from the hand-drawn `M6 12.6l4 4 8-9`.

**Each mark is fitted, not just scaled.** Tabler draws a mark across the whole 24-unit box (a check from 5 to 20); ours
were inset to clear the disc's edge (6 to 18). For each mark the fit (rasterized in Chromium, the ink box of the old mark
against the new) chose a scale and a centre so the Tabler drawing fits inside the old mark's ink box at the old stroke
weight, which the generator divides by the scale so the weight does not change with the size. Measured result, in disc
units: dash, x and the dot cover exactly the old box; the check sits within 0.5 of it; the question mark matches its
width and is 1.45 shorter (11.85 against 13.3); and the slash matches its height and is narrower, because Tabler's slash is steeper than our
45°. That steeper slash on `[/]` is the one visible difference.

**The licence travels.** The drawings are six single strokes, not a substantial portion of the set, and the generated
block names Tabler's MIT licence. The Marp kit's `THIRD-PARTY-LICENSES.txt` already shipped Tabler's full notice for
the icons plugin; its README table now names the marks in `lattice.css` and `lattice-min.css` too, and the docs site's
`third-party-licenses.txt` gains the Tabler entry it lacked (the icons plugin was already shipping Tabler there). The
minified bundle strips comments, as it does for every other note.

**Not changed:** the `--shape-*` chrome masks (arrows, chevrons, refresh, pause, spark, warning) are hand-drawn the same
way, and Tabler has every one of them. They are the same move, left for a separate change so this one stays the marks.
