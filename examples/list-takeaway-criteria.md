# Criteria live in `list` now

`list takeaway numbered · absorbs list-criteria`

A nested gloss under each item stacks a muted reason line under a bold lead, and the number keeps one size.

---

<!-- _class: list takeaway numbered -->
<!-- _footer: "Four criteria · the common case" -->

## Every "verified" names the surface it ran on and carries proof from it.

1. Name the surface
   - The real browser, the real export, the real device.
2. Attach the artifact
   - A screenshot, a log, a measured number from that surface.
3. Say "unverified" out loud
   - When the real surface is out of reach, write it down.
4. Treat "CI is green" as partial
   - It proves only what CI actually runs.

---

<!-- _class: list takeaway numbered -->
<!-- _footer: "Three criteria · the number stays the same size" -->

## Criteria are gates: clear them in order.

1. State the bar
   - Each criterion is a pass-or-fail line, not a preference.
2. Order by veto power
   - The criterion most likely to kill goes first.
3. Keep the list short
   - Three gates decide; six gates stall.

---

<!-- _class: list takeaway numbered -->
<!-- _footer: "Five criteria at the density ceiling · the gloss wraps" -->

## Five gates is the most one decision should clear.

1. The first gate
   - A criterion at the hard budget states the bar and the evidence that clears it, in one breath.
2. The second gate
   - Twenty-two words is the ceiling; this clause is written to sit just under it, deliberately.
3. The third gate
   - Middle gates read fastest — put the contested one here.
4. The fourth gate
   - Attention thins; shorten the clauses as the list descends.
5. The fifth gate
   - The stop. A sixth gate means the bar is a rubric, not a decision.

## Four requirements every decision system must meet.

- Speed
  - Decisions close inside their window.
- Auditability
  - Every decision above the threshold carries a traceable rationale.
- Adoption
  - Weekly use, or calibration never runs.
- Calibration
  - It has to improve over time.
