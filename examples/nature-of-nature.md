# The nature of nature.

`An inquiry, from inside the thing itself`

What is the world, that it should be like this — and never once hold still?

---

<!-- _class: content -->

## Nature is not a collection of things. It is the way things happen.

We name the tree, the river, the storm — and mistake the names for the world. But the tree is a slow fire, the river a standing wave, the storm a knot that unties itself.

Every noun in nature is a verb caught mid-motion.

---

<!-- _class: big-number -->

`Physis · the first word for nature`

## The Greeks heard nature as a verb before it was ever a noun.

- physis
  - From *phúō*, "to grow, to bring forth" — nature named not the things, but their way of coming to be.

---

<!-- _class: cycle -->

`The forest cycle`

## The forest keeps nothing, and loses nothing.

- Seed
  - A life begins from the ruin of the one before it.
- Growth
  - It takes sun, rain, and soil, and stands them up as wood.
- Death
  - The standing life falls; the form it held is done.
- Decay
  - Fungus and worm return it to the ground, and feed the seed.

---

<!-- _class: compare-prose -->

## Nature wears two faces — the making, and the made.

- Natura naturans `nature naturing`
  - The ceaseless process of becoming — nature as the verb, never finished, never still.
- Natura naturata `nature natured`
  - The forms it has thrown up — nature as the noun, every one of them on loan and called back.

---

<!-- _class: quote -->

> No man ever steps in the same river twice — for it is not the same river, and he is not the same man.

`Heraclitus, on a world that is only ever flowing`

---

<!-- _class: cards-grid -->

`Four signatures`

## Nature signs everything it makes with the same four marks.

- Self-similarity
  - The branch repeats the tree; the river's delta repeats the leaf's vein.
- Cycles
  - Nothing is ever spent — only moved, and moved again, and returned.
- Emergence
  - The whole keeps a property no single part was ever holding.
- Impermanence
  - Every form is a loan against time, and time always collects.

---

<!-- _class: content -->

## To understand nature is to stop hunting for the noun.

The question was never "what things is the world made of?" It was "what is the world doing?" — and the answer, everywhere you look, is the same: becoming, and becoming again.

The river is the flowing. The forest is the turning. You are the living, not the thing that lives.

---

<!-- _class: closing silent finish-none -->

## You are not a thing in nature. You are nature, naturing.

`The verb was never something you watch. It is something you are.`
