`Feature demo · narrated delivery`

# The deck now carries how it should be delivered.

A presentation pace that travels with the file, a cursor that points at whatever is being narrated, and a buffered range you can actually see.

---

<!-- _class: statement -->

`The deck's own rhythm`

## This deck asks for a deliberate pace.

Present holds a long beat on every slide. Open it on a machine set to brisk and it still waits about two point two seconds. The rhythm used to live in the viewer's browser, which lost the author's choice the moment the deck was shared.

---

<!-- _class: list-criteria -->

`Resolution order`

## Where a presentation pace comes from, in order.

- A live millisecond override
  - The presenter's adjustment mid-run. Never written into the file.
- The deck's own declaration
  - The author's intent, and it travels with the file.
- The workspace preset
  - The default when a deck declares nothing. Never an override.
- The shipped fallback
  - One point four seconds, when no one has said otherwise.

---

<!-- _class: statement -->

## Turn Guide on and a cursor points at the sentence being spoken.

Captions show the words and the voice speaks them. Guide shows you where to look. Three independent switches over one narration, on any deck, with nothing to author.

---

<!-- _class: statement -->

`One conductor`

## The cursor never runs on its own clock.

Read aloud owns the beat. The cursor moves when the narrated sentence changes and at no other time, so a stall holds it in place rather than drifting it onto a line nobody has reached. When the narration says something the slide does not show, Guide points at nothing rather than guessing.

---

<!-- _class: statement -->

`The rail`

## A buffered range you can see in every palette.

Tone alone tops out near two to one across the thirty six palettes, under the three to one a meaningful signal has to clear. The buffered range is now the played range's own ink, hatched rather than solid, and a pattern is not a color relationship.

---
