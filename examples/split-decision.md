# One choice, one page at a time

`Auto-split · split-compare`

A two-way decision needs the question, both answers and the recommendation. On a tall page it used to keep the question and drop the recommendation.

---

<!-- _class: content -->

## What this deck shows

The run reads title, first option, second option, recommendation. The question rides
every option page, because a comparison read one answer at a time needs the question
beside each.

The recommendation lands once, on its own closing page. It is the component's own
`.verdict` slot, not a page for leftovers — which is why this run always ends on four
pages rather than three.

---

<!-- _class: split-compare -->

`Decision Required`

## Do we run the reviewer queue ourselves, or hand it to Northlake?

Volume triples in March either way; the question is who carries the load.

- Keep it in house
  - Four reviewers to hire, eleven weeks to onboard
  - Our clinical rules stay ours to change
- Hand it to Northlake
  - Live in three weeks against their existing bench
  - Rule changes go through their release train

> Keep it in house. The rules change monthly and a release train would set the pace of our own product.

---

<!-- _class: split-compare -->

`Renewal`

## Do we price the March renewal per record, or flat?

The customer has asked for one number they can put in a budget.

- Per record
  - Tracks the volume we actually review
  - The customer cannot forecast it
- Flat platform fee
  - One number, forecastable, easy to approve
  - We carry the volume risk in a growth year

> Flat, with a volume band. Forecastable for them, capped for us.

---

<!-- _class: content -->

## Why the verdict gets its own page

A recommendation printed beside each option reads as a caption on that option. Printed
once at the end, it reads as the answer to the question the cover asked.

That is also what the un-split portrait page could not do: it kept the two option cards
and pushed the recommendation off the page entirely, with no warning.
