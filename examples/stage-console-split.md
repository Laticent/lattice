# The room gets the deck. You get the cockpit.

`Studio · Stage`

Present now opens two surfaces instead of one: a chrome-free Stage window for the projector, and the console you already knew — with your notes and the next slide on it.

<!-- note: Open on the split. Until now, projecting Present projected the presenter's instruments too. One window later, the room sees a deck and nothing else. -->

<!-- _class: split-compare -->

`What changed`

## Two surfaces, two audiences.

The controls did not move because they were untidy. They moved because they were pointed at the wrong person.

- Stage
  - The slide, filling the display
  - Captions, so a muted room follows
  - One progress rail. No buttons
- Console
  - Play, the lens, the slide grid
  - Next slide and speaker notes
  - A talk clock and private zoom

<!-- note: The rule is one line: if it is aimed at the room it goes to the Stage, and if it is aimed at you it stays on the laptop. Captions and the rail are for the room. Notes never are. -->

## Open, place, talk.

1. Stage
   - *Press S. The deck opens in its own window.*
2. Place
   - *It finds the external display; F fills it.*
3. Talk
   - *Drive from the console. The room only follows.*

<!-- note: Emphasize the one-way wire. Keys pressed on the Stage do nothing at all — an audience is not a second driver, which is exactly what the old second window was. -->

## What came back to the console.

- Next slide.
  - What comes after this one, from the same engine.
- Speaker notes.
  - Your talk track, on the screen only you see.
- Talk clock.
  - Counting since you started. Reset arms first.
- Private zoom.
  - Pinch the console slide. The room sees nothing.

<!-- note: These four are what the retired second window carried. They did not need a window — they needed to be on the presenter's surface, which the overlay already was. -->
