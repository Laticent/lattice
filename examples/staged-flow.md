# The case, built one stage at a time.

Three new `list-steps` variants for a labelled argument that cascades **down**
the frame — a chevron cascade, a converging funnel, and an editorial ghost —
all from the same plain numbered list.

---

<!-- _class: list-steps chevron -->

## `chevron` — make the case, stage by stage.

1. Problem
   - Define the problem that is causing the pain.
2. Vision
   - Show what the world looks like once it is solved.
3. Approach
   - Detail the moves that get from here to there.
4. Plan
   - Commit to concrete steps, owners, and a date.

---

<!-- _class: list-steps converge -->

## `converge` — four signals narrow onto one bet.

1. Market
   - A real, growing pool of people feel this pain weekly.
2. Wedge
   - One underserved job we can win before anyone notices.
3. Moat
   - The advantage that compounds once we are in.
4. Bet
   - The single wager the next two quarters are built around.

---

<!-- _class: list-steps ghost -->

## `ghost` — the thesis, stated plainly.

1. Belief
   - Distribution beats features in a crowded market.
2. Consequence
   - So we spend on reach before we spend on polish.
3. Risk
   - A better-funded copy of us outspends the channel.
4. Guardrail
   - We defend the channel the moment retention proves out.

---

<!-- _class: list-steps chevron -->

## The chevron holds at three stages, too.

1. Land
   - Win one team with a sharp, self-serve first use.
2. Expand
   - Turn that team into the account's internal reference.
3. Defend
   - Wire in so switching costs outlast the next budget cycle.

---

<!-- _class: statement -->

## Same list, three arguments.

Every slide here is a plain numbered list with a one-line body — the variant
name (`chevron`, `converge`, `ghost`) is the only thing that changes. Pick
`chevron` for a general downward sequence, `converge` when the stages narrow
onto a decision, and `ghost` when the words are the point.
