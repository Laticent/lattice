# The virtue of virtue.

`A quiet argument, in the season of blossoms`

Why goodness is worth choosing for its own sake — and for no other.

---

<!-- _class: content -->

## Virtue is not a means to something else — it is the thing itself.

We prize courage because it protects us, honesty because it earns trust. But strip away every reward, and a virtuous act still shines.

The blossom does not bloom to be admired. It blooms because that is its nature.

---

<!-- _class: big-number -->

`Aristotle · Nicomachean Ethics`

## Flourishing is not virtue's reward — it is virtue in action.

- eudaimonia
  - The good life *is* the virtuous life, lived — not a prize handed to the good, but the very shape of their days.

---

<!-- _class: cards-grid -->

`The cardinal four`

## The classical virtues are not rules — they are ways of seeing.

- Courage
  - Acting well in the presence of fear, not its absence.
- Temperance
  - Wanting the right things, in the right measure.
- Justice
  - Giving each their due, including the stranger.
- Wisdom
  - Knowing which virtue this moment asks of you.

---

<!-- _class: quote -->

> We are what we repeatedly do. Excellence, then, is not an act, but a habit.

`Attributed to Aristotle, on the second nature of the good`

---

<!-- _class: content -->

## Do good for a reward, and you have not been good — you have been paid.

The moment virtue becomes a transaction, it stops being virtue. The honest merchant who is honest only because it sells will lie the instant lying sells better.

Character is what remains when no one is keeping score.

---

<!-- _class: compare-prose -->

## Two people keep a promise — only one of them is good.

- The merchant `keeps it for the deal`
  - Weighs the cost, finds it profitable, and pays. Change the price and the promise breaks.
- The gardener `keeps it for the promise`
  - Tends it because it is theirs to tend. No audit, no audience, no exception.

---

<!-- _class: content -->

## Character is a garden — it is grown, not granted.

No one is born just, only capable of justice. Virtue is cultivation: small acts, repeated in season, until the good is not a decision but a disposition.

This is the patient art the East calls self-cultivation, and the blossom its yearly proof.

---

<!-- _class: closing silent finish-none -->

## Be good for nothing — and you will have everything.

`Seneca said it plainer: virtue is its own reward.`
